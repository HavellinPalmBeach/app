## A. What a best-in-class contents inventory and valuation package contains

1. **Every article at the property, listed room by room and named specifically.** Articles in one room may be grouped only where none of them is worth more than $100, and each named article gets its own value. This is the federal estate-tax standard ([26 CFR §20.2031-6(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2031-6); [GPO copy](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2014-title26-vol14/pdf/CFR-2014-title26-vol14-sec20-2031-6.pdf)) [P]. Florida asks only for "reasonable detail" ([§733.604(1)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/733.604)) [P], so the federal standard is the defensible ceiling even where no Form 706 is due.
2. **For each item, the estimated fair market value at the date of death.** This is required by [§733.604(1)](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/733.604) [P] and [Fla. Prob. R. 5.340(a)](https://state-rules.com/florida/probate/5.340) [P]. Protected homestead is listed and designated but not valued (5.340(a), as reproduced in the [Bar's SC2023-1661 notice](https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2024/01/SC2023-1661_011024_Publication-Notice.pdf)) [P]. Under an alternate-valuation election, property sold or distributed within six months is valued at the disposition date, and anything still held at six months is valued at the six-month date ([26 USC §2032](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2032)) [P].
3. **The definition of value stated, and the market chosen for each line.**
   - FMV is the price between a willing buyer and a willing seller. It is not a forced-sale price, and it is not the price in a market other than the one where the item is most commonly sold to the public. For an item the public buys at retail, the value is the retail price, not what a dealer would pay ([§20.2031-1(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2031-1); identical gift-tax text at [§25.2512-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/25.2512-1)) [S for the estate text, P for the gift text].
   - The most common market is not necessarily the market where the item would fetch the most ([ISA, "Choosing the Right Market"](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/365/choosing-the-right-market)) [S].
   - ISA names four markets (retail, wholesale, orderly liquidation, forced liquidation), notes that one report may use different markets for different items, and recommends stating which fees are included or excluded ([ISA](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/303/what-kind-of-shape-is-your-report-in); [ISA on fees](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/541/what-fees-should-i-include-in-fair-market-value-and-replacement-value)) [S].
4. **The buyer's premium inside FMV wherever auction is the right market.** The Tax Court so held in *Estate of Scull*, T.C. Memo. 1994-211, and the IRS took the same position in PLR 9235005 ([ISA](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/541/what-fees-should-i-include-in-fair-market-value-and-replacement-value); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/x/317934/music+arts/Valuing+Artwork+for+Federal+Taxation+Purposes+Income+Estate+Gift+Tax+Issues)) [S]. Each comparable should therefore say whether its price is the hammer price or includes the premium.
5. **A stated basis for every value.** On written request a beneficiary must be given a written explanation of how an inventory value was determined, or a copy of the appraisal if one was obtained. Residuary beneficiaries and intestate heirs are entitled for every asset; other beneficiaries only for assets distributed, or proposed to be distributed, to them. The PR must tell each beneficiary of this right ([§733.604(3)](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xlii-estates-and-trusts/fl-st-sect-733-604/)) [P]. The rule adds that interested persons may examine the appraisals behind the inventory values ([R. 5.340](https://www.courtrules.net/florida/florida-probate/rule-5-340)) [P; check the subdivision letter].
6. **An expert appraisal under oath where it is required.** It is required when articles of "marked artistic or intrinsic value" total more than $3,000. The regulation's examples, as I recall the text, are jewelry, furs, silverware, books, statuary, vases, oriental rugs, and coin or stamp collections [U for the exact list]. The appraisal comes with the executor's statement, under penalties of perjury, on the completeness of the list and on the appraiser's disinterested character and qualifications ([§20.2031-6(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2031-6)) [P]. The current Form 706 instructions (Rev. July 2026) require an appraisal under oath, with a statement of qualifications, for any item or collection of similar items valued above $3,000 ([i706](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i706.pdf)) [P]. The 2008 Schedule F used $10,000 for collections; the current draft uses $3,000 ([draft Schedule F](https://www.stayexempt.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/f706sf--dft.pdf)) [P].
7. **Independent appraisers.** Experts must be "reputable and of recognized competency" in the class of property (§20.2031-6(d), quoted by [J.S. Held](https://jsheld.com/insights/articles/the-essential-role-of-qualified-personal-property-appraisers-in-estate-and-donation-valuations)) [S]. The fee must not depend on the value: USPAP's Ethics Rule ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comp_check)) [S], and for donations [§1.170A-17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-17) [P]. The appraiser must have no interest in consigning or buying the items (*Kollsman*, *Rothko*; see C).
8. **USPAP-compliant appraisal reports** under Standards 7 (development) and 8 (reporting), with a signed certification under SR 8-3. The current edition is 2024, effective January 1, 2024, with no fixed expiry ([ISA, Jan. 2026](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/536/uspap-in-2026-what-appraisers-need-to-know)) [S]. Under SR 7-5(b) the appraiser analyses prior sales "and other transfers" of the subject ([2024 Summary of Actions](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/dos/department-and-offices/bpoa/certified-real-estate-appraisers/2024-Summary-of-Actions-for-2024-USPAP-6.02.2023.pdf)) [S].
9. **Photographs of each item and close-ups of its marks.** The IRS asks for a high-quality photograph of donated art worth $20,000 or more ([Pub. 561, as quoted](https://culturalheritagelawyer.com/?p=348)) [S]. No rule requires photographs for estates. They are, however, the best evidence of identity and condition, and of what was found.
10. **A way to amend or supplement the inventory.** An amendment is owed when property is found missing from it, when a value or description proves "erroneous or misleading", or when the value of an item listed as unknown is later determined ([§733.604(2)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/733.604) [P]; [R. 5.340](https://ruledex.com/florida/florida-probate-rules/part-ii-probate/rule-5-340-inventory/) [P]).
11. **Confidential handling.** Filed inventories are confidential. They are disclosed only to the PR, the PR's attorney, an interested person under §731.201, or on a court order for good cause ([§733.604(1)(b)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/733.604)) [P].
12. **Exempt property identified and subtotalled.** It covers household furniture, furnishings and appliances in the usual home up to a net value of $20,000 at death, and two motor vehicles of no more than 15,000 lb gross weight each. The rights are waived unless a petition is filed by the later of 4 months after service of the notice of administration or 40 days after the end of a will contest ([§732.402](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/732.402)) [P].
13. **Specific devises and any §732.515 list matched to item numbers.** Such a list must be signed by the testator and describe the items and devisees with "reasonable certainty". The most recent writing controls, and property used in a trade or business is excluded ([§732.515](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/732.515)) [P].
14. **Written approval before anything leaves, by the person with authority.** Unless the will provides otherwise, co-PRs must all concur on every act of administration and distribution (§733.615) [U]. Co-trustees may act by majority (§736.0703) [U].
15. **No purchase by the fiduciary's agents, and every conflict disclosed.** A sale to the PR's agent or attorney, or any transaction "affected by a conflict of interest", can be voided by any interested person who did not consent after fair disclosure ([§733.610](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/0733.610)) [P]. The trustee's equivalent is the duty of loyalty ([§736.0802](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/736.0802)) [P].
16. **No sale or distribution ahead of an IRS investigation on a 706 estate,** unless the IRS has first been told and given a sworn appraisal ([§20.2031-6(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2031-6)) [P].
17. **Every line reconciled from its date-of-death value to what became of it and what it brought,** so that the accounting can show all property transactions, gains and losses, and agents' compensation ([R. 5.346](https://www.courtrules.net/florida/florida-probate/rule-5-346); [§736.08135(2)(b)](https://www.myfloridahouse.gov/Statutes/2019/0736.08135/)) [P].
18. **Fees that are transparent and can be reviewed.** The court can review the employment and pay of any agent the PR hires ([§733.6175](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2016/0733.6175)) [P]. The burden of showing the fees reasonable falls on the PR and the agent, and refunds can be ordered ([R. 5.355](https://state-rules.com/florida/probate/5.355)) [S/P].
19. **Donation substantiation that is correct for whoever claims the deduction** (see B5).
20. **Vendor due diligence a bank trust department can file.** This covers insurance (fidelity bond, liability, property, cyber), the backgrounds of key personnel, subcontractors, physical security and incident reporting ([Interagency TPRM Guidance, OCC Bulletin 2023-17](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2023/bulletin-2023-17.html) [P for its existence]; factor list via [Guidehouse](https://guidehouse.com/insights/financial-services/2023/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-risk-management) [S]). A 2026 Federal Reserve docket suggests the guidance is being revised ([FR-2026-0031](https://www.federalreserve.gov/apps/proposals/comments/FR-2026-0031-01-C04)) [S]. The OCC's booklet *Unique and Hard-to-Value Assets* (2012) covers tangible assets and collectibles in fiduciary accounts and requires "sound fiduciary principles" ([OCC](https://occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/unique-hard-to-value-assets/pub-ch-unique.pdf)) [P for scope; its specific inspection, appraisal and insurance text is U].

## B. What each paper must do

### B1. Court inventory (§733.604; Rule 5.340)
- **Who and when.** The PR files a verified inventory within 60 days after letters issue, unless one is already on file. The court may extend the time for cause, but the petition and order must still be served ([R. 5.340(a)–(b)](https://state-rules.com/florida/probate/5.340)) [P].
- **Contents.** Each item listed in reasonable detail with its estimated FMV at death. Protected homestead is listed and designated but not valued [P].
- **Service.** The inventory and every amendment and supplement go to the surviving spouse, each heir at law (intestate), each residuary beneficiary (testate), and anyone who asks in writing ([R. 5.340(d)](https://ruledex.com/florida/florida-probate-rules/part-ii-probate/rule-5-340-inventory/)) [P; current copies online drop paragraphs, so confirm against the [Bar's October 2026 rules PDF](https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/10/Probate-Rules-10-15-26-1.pdf)].
- **Elective estate.** An inventory of the elective estate must value its assets "as required by law" (§732.2055). This was amended effective October 1, 2024 ([SC2023-1661](https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/2024/sc2023-1661.html)) [P].
- **Safe-deposit boxes.** Their inventory is a separate rule, [R. 5.342](https://ruledex.com/florida/florida-probate-rules/part-ii-probate/rule-5-342-inventory-of-safe-deposit-box/) [P that it exists].
- **No Rule 5.3461 exists** in the current rules ([index](https://state-rules.com/florida/probate/)) [S].
- **What Havellin supplies.** The tangible-property schedule the PR verifies and counsel files. It must stand up to a §733.604(3) request line by line. "Estimated" FMV means Florida does not require an appraisal for every item, but every estimate needs a basis Havellin can explain in writing.

### B2. Trust accounting schedule (§736.0813; §736.08135)
- **When.** A trustee of an irrevocable trust accounts to each qualified beneficiary at least annually, on termination and on a change of trustee. A beneficiary may waive accountings in writing ([§736.0813](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/736.0813)) [P].
- **Contents.** The accounting must:
  - be a "reasonably understandable report";
  - identify the trust, the trustee and the period;
  - show all cash and property transactions and all significant transactions, including the compensation of the trustee **and the trustee's agents**, realised gains and losses, and every receipt and disbursement;
  - to the extent feasible, identify and value the assets on hand with **two values**: the acquisition or carrying value and the estimated current value;
  - list known liabilities;
  - in a final accounting, include a plan of distribution for undistributed assets.

  See [§736.08135(2)](https://www.myfloridahouse.gov/Statutes/2019/0736.08135/) [P]. I recall that it must also name each noncash asset that has no reasonably reliable estimate of value [U].
- **Why adequate disclosure matters.** A disclosure document that adequately discloses a matter starts a six-month bar on claims about it ([§736.1008(2)](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/736.1008)) [P]. A clear, valued and reconciled contents schedule is therefore what protects the trustee. A vague one leaves the claim period open.
- **What Havellin supplies.** For each line: the carrying value (normally the value at death; see D6), the current estimate, the disposition, the date, the gross, the fees, the net, and the payee. Havellin's own compensation, and any vendor fees, must be traceable as agents' compensation.

### B3. Estate accounting (Rule 5.346)
It covers all cash and property transactions since the last accounting, plus a schedule of assets at the end of the period. It must be understandable to a reader who does not know estate jargon, open with a concise summary, and carry enough information to put interested persons on notice of every significant transaction ([R. 5.346](https://www.courtrules.net/florida/florida-probate/rule-5-346)) [P]. One copy also requires two values and gains and losses shown separately ([floridarules.net](https://floridarules.net/?p=843)) [S: the published versions conflict, so verify].

### B4. Item description and valuation statement
**Item description.** Assembled from the IRS's art-description list (size, subject, medium, artist or culture, approximate date, history and authenticity, a photograph: [Pub. 561 via JofA](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2016/apr/importance-of-appraisals-when-gifting-art.html)) [S], ISA's "complete and accurate property description written to industry standards" ([ISA](https://isa-appraisers.org/find-an-appraiser/be-certain-of-its-value)) [S], and USPAP SR 7-2's requirement to identify the characteristics that establish the property's identity [U for the exact text]. Each description should carry:
- the object;
- the maker or artist, with the qualifying term defined in a glossary;
- the title, pattern or model;
- the date or *circa*, and the origin;
- the material and technique (an original or a print, sterling or plate, hand-knotted or machine-made, the karat);
- dimensions and weight (troy ounces, carats);
- every mark, signature, label or edition number, quoted exactly;
- condition, specifically, and whether the item was inspected;
- provenance and papers;
- for a set, its count and composition;
- the room, photo references and a permanent item number.

**The attribution ladder, from most to least certain.** *By*, then *attributed to* (probably by), *studio or workshop of*, *circle of* (a contemporary associate), *school or follower of*, *manner of* (in the style, later), and *after* (a copy). The wording differs from house to house ([artnet glossary](https://news.artnet.com/market/glossary-old-master-attributions-1594426)) [S]. A guess by Agent One should be written in this vocabulary, never as a flat attribution.

**Valuation statement.** Following USPAP SR 8-2 and SR 8-3 [U for exact text; structure via the [ABI guidelines](https://www.abi.org/abi-journal/personal-property-appraisal-report-guidelines) and the [Standard 8 copy](https://appraisalengine.com/wp-content/uploads/USPAP-STANDARD-8-PERSONAL-PROPERTY-APPRAISAL.pdf)] [S], it should state:
- the client and intended users;
- the intended use (probate inventory, Form 706, equitable division, sale planning);
- the type and definition of value, with its citation;
- the effective date (the date of death) and the date of the report;
- the scope of work and whether each item was inspected;
- the market chosen for each class, and why;
- the comparables: venue, sale date, lot, hammer price or price with premium, condition, link; sold prices only, never asking prices ([ISA FMV list](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/516/part-iii-fair-market-value-guidance-list)) [S];
- every extraordinary assumption (for example, "authenticity assumed") and every hypothetical condition;
- the reconciliation of the evidence;
- a signed certification: no interest in the property; no fee contingent on value; services performed on the property within the prior three years disclosed ([ISA FAQ](https://isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/468/uspap-frequently-asked-questions)) [S]; inspection disclosed; significant assistance named.

**An AI estimate is not an appraisal.** Label it an estimate, give its basis, and route every item that crosses the §20.2031-6(b) or Form 706 threshold to a credentialed appraiser. An appraiser whose work produces a substantial or gross valuation misstatement faces a penalty. It is the lesser of (a) the greater of 10% of the underpayment or $1,000, and (b) 125% of the appraiser's fee. It is waived where the value was "more likely than not" proper ([§6695A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6695A)) [P].

### B5. Donation receipt
- **The charity's acknowledgment.** A contribution of $250 or more needs a contemporaneous written acknowledgment. It must give a description, **but not the value**, of property other than cash; say whether any goods or services were given in return, describing and estimating any that were; and arrive by the earlier of the filing date or the due date including extensions ([§170(f)(8)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-regs/td8623.txt)) [S quoting the statute]. Content failures are fatal: *15 West 17th St. LLC* lost a $64.5 million deduction for want of the goods-or-services statement ([Arnold & Porter](https://arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2017/02/a-charitable-gift-receipt-is-worth-a)) [S].
- **Appraisal and Form 8283.** Form 8283 is required above $500. Above $5,000 for one item or a group of similar items, a qualified appraisal and Section B, signed by the appraiser and the donee, are required ([ISA on 8283](https://isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/401/ask-an-instructor-irs-form-8283)) [S]. For art of $20,000 or more, the signed appraisal is attached ([as quoted](https://culturalheritagelawyer.com/?p=348)) [S]. A qualified appraisal follows the substance of USPAP; the appraiser's fee may not be based on the value; and the appraisal must be received before the return is due ([§1.170A-17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-17)) [P].
- **Who claims the deduction.** An estate's income-tax charitable deduction under §642(c) reaches only amounts paid out of gross income under the governing instrument. A gift traceable to principal earns no deduction ([ACTEC Foundation](https://actecfoundation.org/podcasts/charitable-deductions-and-section-642c/); [PLR 202618006](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/202618006.pdf)) [S]. So an estate's donation of household contents probably yields no estate deduction, and whether a beneficiary may claim it after distribution is for counsel (D7).
- **What Havellin's Donation Record does.** It ties the charity's receipt to item numbers. It never prints a value as the charity's, and it never claims anybody's deduction.

### B6. Release approval
There is no statutory form. The paper derives from fiduciary law:
- **Authority.** A PR may sell without a court order unless the will or a court order says otherwise, and may employ appraisers and other agents, including associated ones, whose fees count against the PR's own compensation ([§733.612(19)](https://flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2025/733.612)) [P for (19); the sale power U].
- **Who must sign.** Every co-PR, unless the will says otherwise (§733.615) [U]; co-trustees by majority (§736.0703) [U].
- **Conflicts.** Consent after fair disclosure is what makes a conflicted transaction stand ([§733.610](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/0733.610)) [P].
- **Reasonable care with agents.** A trustee must take reasonable care in choosing an agent, setting the terms of the delegation and reviewing the agent's work ([§736.0807](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2022/736.0807)) [P]. The signed request, with what it lists, is that review on paper.

**What the paper should show:** the items by number with their values; the destination and the taker; any specific devise, §732.515 list, exempt-property claim or dispute; the 706 status; who works for Havellin; and every fiduciary's signature and date.

## C. Failure modes and how a best-in-class process prevents each

1. **Values understated, or taken at the wrong market level.**
   - *How it happens.* Dealer buy prices, estate-sale tag prices or forced-sale figures are used where retail or auction is the most common market, and the auction premium is left out.
   - *What it costs.* §20.2031-1(b) and *Scull* (A3, A4). The penalty is 20% where the value claimed is 65% or less of the correct value, and 40% at 40% or less, once the underpayment attributable to it exceeds $5,000 ([§6662(g), (h)](https://m.openjurist.org/26/usc/6662)) [S/P].
   - *Evidence.* The Art Advisory Panel (FY2016) adjusted 60% of the 555 items it reviewed, a net 17% upward. The IRS has long seen non-charitable (estate and gift) values as low ([WealthManagement on the IRS report](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/high-net-worth/irs-art-advisory-panel-issues-2017-annual-report); [Pub. 5392](https://irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p5392--2018.pdf)) [S].
   - *Florida exposure.* A PR owes the duty of an express trustee, and a successful breach claim carries attorney's fees ([§733.609](https://www.myfloridahouse.gov/Statutes/2017/0733.609/)) [P].
   - *Prevention.* Name the market for each line; cite only sold comparables; state the premium treatment; avoid both understating and overstating. A higher value at death also raises the heirs' basis (§1014) [U].
2. **Items missed.** Typical misses are a storage unit, a safe-deposit box, closed drawers, items on loan, and items scheduled on the homeowner's policy. Charges on bank and card statements and the policy's schedule reveal them ([WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/estate-planning/practical-steps-for-dealing-with-artwork-and-tangible-personal-property-in-an-estate)) [S]. *Prevention:* an as-found pass through every room with drawers and closets opened; reconciliation against the insurance schedule; a standing supplementary-inventory route (§733.604(2)).
3. **Reproductions misidentified, attributions overstated, condition misread.** Typical errors are a print read as an original, plate as sterling, costume jewelry as fine, *after* read as *by*. In *Kollsman* the estate's Sotheby's expert exaggerated how dirt affected a painting's value, offered no comparables, and could not explain why one painting later sold for five times his figure; the IRS expert prevailed. The decision is T.C. Memo. 2017-40, affirmed by the Ninth Circuit in 2019 in an unpublished memorandum ([GiftLaw](https://plannedgiving.mssu.edu/giftlawpro/glawpro_cases.jsp?ID=495)) [S]. *Prevention:* close-ups of marks; the attribution glossary; hedged names; a specialist for anything with a mark or signature; authenticity stated as an extraordinary assumption until it is confirmed.
4. **Items counted twice or lost.** The same object is photographed from two angles, or a line vanishes between the inventory and its disposition. *Prevention:* permanent item numbers; a duplicate review; and a close-out rule that every line ends in exactly one disposition backed by evidence (a sale statement, a signed receipt, a donation receipt or a distribution receipt).
5. **Values with no stated basis.** The PR then cannot answer a §733.604(3) request or defend the figure in an examination, and the appraisal is open to attack (*Kollsman*'s missing comparables). *Prevention:* a basis and comparables on every line, and the appraisal linked to its lines.
6. **Stale or wrong dates.** The value is given as of the inspection or the sale rather than the death, or a later sale price is used without adjusting for the market. *Prevention:* print the effective date on every schedule. An actual sale soon after death is evidence of value, adjusted for time and for any premium. Apply the §2032 rules where the election is made.
7. **Conflicts of interest.**
   - In *Rothko*, executors sold and consigned the estate's paintings to a gallery in which they had interests and approved the deals without independent appraisals. They were removed, the contracts were voided and appreciation damages were imposed: *Matter of Rothko*, 43 N.Y.2d 305 (1977) ([NY Courts](https://www.nycourts.gov/REPORTER/archives/rothko.htm)) [S].
   - In *Kollsman*, the appraiser was courting the consignment [S].
   - Florida makes conflicted transactions voidable (§733.610, §736.0802).
   - *Prevention:* no purchase by staff, contractors or appraisers; appraisers independent of every buyer and consignee; referral fees disclosed in writing; fees never tied to value (USPAP; §1.170A-17).
8. **Disposal before it is allowed.** Things go before the letters issue; before the exempt-property window (4 months) or the specific devises are resolved; before the sworn appraisal or IRS notice on a 706 estate (§20.2031-6(c)); or without every co-PR's concurrence. *Prevention:* written approval keyed to item numbers, and the paper's cautions (B6).
9. **Proceeds that do not reconcile.** A sale net of commissions with no itemised statement leaves the accounting unable to show gains, losses or agents' compensation (§736.08135(2)(b); R. 5.346). *Prevention:* an itemised settlement statement from every channel, reconciled line by line to the cent.
10. **A lump-sum "household contents" line on an estate that holds valuables.** It fails §20.2031-6 and invites both a challenge and an IRS adjustment. It also gives beneficiaries nothing to divide or equalise. *Prevention:* itemise; group only items under $100.
11. **Confidentiality broken.** The inventory is circulated beyond the PR, counsel and interested persons, or its photographs are published (§733.604(1)(b)). A beneficiary who is not residuary is entitled only to their own assets' basis (§733.604(3)(b)). *Prevention:* share the package with counsel and fiduciaries only; send beneficiary receipts per beneficiary; publish no photographs of a contested matter.
12. **Donations not properly substantiated.** A receipt that states a value, leaves out the goods-or-services statement or names the wrong donor (B5). *Prevention:* describe without valuing; name the donor deliberately.
13. **Agents' fees challenged.** The court reviews them under §733.6175, the agent bears the burden of proving them reasonable, and refunds can be ordered (R. 5.355). A recent Fifth DCA opinion holds that a surcharge measures the actual loss caused, no more (*Kim Law v. Williams*, 2026; [summary](https://flexlaw.co/case/3394738/2026-kim-law-v-williams-iv)) [S: a machine summary, so read the opinion]. *Prevention:* a written fee basis; hours and receipts kept; vendor fees passed through and disclosed.
14. **Blockage ignored or misapplied.** Large holdings of similar items can be discounted, but the courts limit it: *Estate of O'Keeffe* rejected a flat 75% discount and applied it by tier ([itsartlaw](https://itsartlaw.org/case-review/blockage-discounts-and-artists-estates-the-de-kooning-post-mortem); [ISA](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/522/what-blockage)) [S]. Ordinary household quantities rarely qualify. *Prevention:* value a set at its count, and leave any discount to an appraiser.

## D. Open legal questions for Florida counsel

1. **AI estimates.** Is a value estimated with AI help, with a written basis, and labelled "estimated", adequate for a §733.604(1) inventory and as the §733.604(3) "written explanation"? Above what value should an independent appraiser value instead, if any value short of the §20.2031-6(b) and Form 706 $3,000 tests? Must the PR's verification say who made the estimates?
2. **Grouping.** Does the 15th Judicial Circuit (Palm Beach) accept federal-style grouping (articles under $100) as "reasonable detail"? Does it accept a lump line for ordinary contents on a non-taxable estate?
3. **Exempt property.** Should household items within the §732.402 class be held until the petition deadline passes or a waiver is signed? How is "net value" measured, and what if the $20,000 cap is exceeded? The current statute covers two vehicles.
4. **Who must approve a release.** Do co-PRs need unanimous concurrence for each release (§733.615)? Is a majority of co-trustees enough (§736.0703), or should the release request still collect every signature? Both sections are unverified here.
5. **Havellin as the fiduciary's agent.** Does §733.610 or §736.0802 reach Havellin's contractors and their families? Are vendor referral fees a "conflict of interest" needing consent after fair disclosure, and does the agreement's disclosure suffice? Must referral fees appear in a trust accounting as agents' compensation (§736.08135(2)(b))? Is Havellin's fee open to §733.6175 review?
6. **The Trust Schedule.** What carrying value should tangible property received by a trust at death carry: the value at death, or something else? Will a Havellin schedule count as "adequate disclosure" that starts the §736.1008(2) six months?
7. **Donations.** Who should be the donor of an estate's household goods: the estate, under §642(c), or beneficiaries after distribution? Should the receipt name the estate or the beneficiary? Should Havellin advise distributing to the beneficiaries before donating where a deduction matters?
8. **Form 706 estates.** Should Havellin's appraisers sign under oath per §20.2031-6(b)? Should the PR give the §20.2031-6(c) notice before any sale, and where does that notice go now that district directors no longer exist?
9. **The buyer's premium in a Florida inventory.** Should a non-tax probate inventory include it where auction is the most common market? No Florida authority on this was found.
10. **Disclosure.** May the package and the photographs sit on Google Drive and go by email to counsel and fiduciaries? Who are the "interested persons" (§731.201) entitled to the inventory? Does sending values to a non-residuary beneficiary exceed §733.604(3)(b)?
11. **Elective share.** When does the October 2024 amendment to Rule 5.340, which values elective-estate assets as §732.2055 requires, call for a separate valuation of the contents?
12. **Uncertain rule text.** Current online copies of Rule 5.340 (service and amendment paragraphs) and Rule 5.346 (the two values) conflict. Confirm them against the Bar's October 2026 PDF.
