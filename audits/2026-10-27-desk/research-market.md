## A. Competitors in Palm Beach County, and the opening

### A.1 Who does contents valuation here, and what they offer

### A.2 The rules every reader of the inventory judges it against

- **Treas. Reg. §20.2031-6.**
  - A room-by-room list is "desirable".
  - Articles may be grouped only within one room, and only where none is worth more than $100.
  - Where art, jewelry, collections and the like exceed $3,000 in total, an expert's appraisal under oath goes with the return ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2031-6)).
  - Paintings must be described by size, subject and artist ([Rev. Proc. 96-15](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/rp96_15.pdf)).
- **Florida.**
  - The verified inventory is due within 60 days of letters, with an estimated date-of-death value for each item ([Rule 5.340](https://ruledex.com/florida/florida-probate-rules/part-ii-probate/rule-5-340-inventory/)).
  - An appraisal is optional, but a beneficiary may demand in writing "an explanation of how a value was set" ([§733.604](https://www.syfert.com/florida/statutes/0733.604.html)). A per-line valuation basis answers that demand.
- **Which market.**
  - Fair market value is set in the market where the item is "most commonly sold to the public". For most estate property that is generally the auction market, and asking prices are not completed sales ([ISA, "Choosing the Right Market"](https://isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/365)).
  - The IRS has ruled that an auction value includes the buyer's premium (Letter Ruling 9235005, binding only on that taxpayer, as described in [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/x/317934/music+arts/Valuing+Artwork+for+Federal+Taxation+Purposes+Income+Estate+Gift+Tax+Issues) and [WealthManagement](https://wealthmanagement.com/node/34113)). An ISA post says the same, unless the purpose is the seller's net ([ISA](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/524/after-hours-office-hours-recap-september-2025)).
- **Independence.** In *Estate of Kollsman*, the Tax Court rejected an auction specialist's values because he hoped to win the consignment. The court called it a "significant conflict of interest" ([J.S. Held](https://www.jsheld.com/insights/articles/the-essential-role-of-qualified-personal-property-appraisers-in-estate-and-donation-valuations); [Valuation Review](https://www.valuationreview.com/VR/ArticlesVR/76873.aspx)). Auction houses still rebate appraisal fees on consignment ([Heritage sample agreement](https://wine.ha.com/c/s/d/sample-appraisal-agreement.pdf)).

### A.3 The opening

1. **The whole house, not the top five per cent.**
   - Auction houses appraise what they can sell, and liquidators value what they will sell, at a margin.
   - Nobody local sells a photographed, room-by-room list of everything, grouped under §20.2031-6's $100 rule, with the $3,000 aggregate tracked and appraisers brought in by rule.
   - Disputes over tangible property are among the commonest causes of estate conflict, according to practitioner commentary ([Wiggin](https://www.wiggin.com/publication/a-guide-to-estate-planning-for-tangible-assets/); [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/estate-planning/dividing-stuff-shouldn-t-be-afterthought)). A complete photographed list is the defence.
2. **Inside the 60 days.** Competitors quote "a timeline with the proposal" (Heritage) or weeks. A full-house inventory with values delivered in days, and with interim copies, fits Rule 5.340's clock.
3. **A stated basis on every line**, which answers §733.604(3)'s demand before it is made. No competitor's marketing promises per-line evidence for everyday lots.
4. **Reconciliation.** A ledger that ties every line to where it went and what it fetched is something no auction house or liquidator offers for the whole house.
5. **Independence, stated carefully.** Havellin never buys, and takes no part of the proceeds, but it does take disclosed vendor referral fees. Under *Kollsman*'s logic, a value set by someone who profits from the channel can be attacked. **Recommendation:** value before the channel is chosen, record the value and its date, and keep the appraiser route for anything that matters.

The threat to watch is Winston Artory: appraisers plus a 50-million-record database, with an office in Palm Beach Gardens. Its scope is art and collections, though, not a whole house.

## B. Software and AI in the space

**Home and insurance inventory tools.**
- **Encircle** uses AI to write item descriptions, brands and model numbers from one photo (it claims "50% or more" less typing). Replacement values are linked to retailers for checking ([Encircle](https://getencircle.com/contents)).
- **HomeZada's Zada AI** lists every object in a room photo, with a value for each and an insurance category ([HomeZada](https://zen.homezada.com/2024/02/19/homezada-launches-ai-vision-for-easiest-and-most-powerful-home-inventory-application/)).
- **Verisk XactContents** has "AutoPricing" from a replacement-cost database, and object recognition in ContentsTrack ([Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/en-ca/insurance/products/xactcontents/multi-object-recognition)).
- **Collector Systems** manages family offices' collections, with appraised and insured values ([Collector Systems](https://www.collectorsystems.com/solutions/corporations-family-offices/)).
- All of these price **replacement cost** (the insurance basis), not fair market value.
- Nothing was found on Sortly's or Itemizer's AI features.

**Estate administration platforms.**
- **EstateExec** charges $199 per estate and tracks an asset inventory ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10002858/EstateExec/)).
- **Estateably** tracks inventory and the accounting ([Estateably](https://estateably.com/product-estate-administration)).
- **Empathy's** help with contents is a human service: its care team helps with "taking inventory" and "finding professionals like appraisers and home liquidators" ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/israeli-startup-empathy-is-on-a-mission-to-support-bereaved-families-with-tech)).
- **ClearEstate** is a service in Canada and some US states ([Advisor.ca](https://www.advisor.ca/tax/estate-planning/digital-tools-make-life-easier-for-executors/)).
- None of them identifies or values an item. They are where a Havellin export would land.

**Appraisers' software.** Wavebid's Appraisal Builder Pro handles data collection, report assembly and a searchable database of the appraiser's own past values. No AI features were found ([Wavebid](https://blog.wavebid.com/wavebid-unveils-new-software-to-streamline-personal-property-appraisals)).

**AI identification and valuation.**
- **Google Lens** is a "reasonably good first step" on patterns, hallmarks and furniture, and weak on reproductions ([Appraisily](https://appraisily.com/antique-identification-and-value-app/)) (unconfirmed: affiliate site).
- **eBay's Magical Listing** writes a listing from one photo and suggests a price from eBay transactions. eBay published no measure of price accuracy, and a seller guide says it "can at times misidentify items" ([eBay](https://innovation.ebayinc.com/tech/features/magical-listing-tool-harnesses-the-power-of-ai-to-make-selling-on-ebay-faster-easier-and-more-accurate/); [3DSellers](https://www.3dsellers.com/blog/ebay-magical-listing-tool)).
- **WorthPoint** has a maker's-marks database. A photo search was planned and is not confirmed to have shipped ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1472512649); [VAR](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/worthpoint-launches-equity-crowdfunding-campaign/)).
- **Cataloguing tools**, such as Estimint ("price estimates in seconds") and AuctionWriter ("NOT a substitute for an official appraisal"), publish no accuracy figures ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.ie/software/1085850/Estimint); [AuctionWriter](https://auctionwriter.com/what-if-you-could-take-a-picture-to-see-how-much-something-is-worth)).
- **LiveArt** sells an art-records API with an AI estimate on each record, "min, mid, max with confidence intervals" ([LiveArt API](https://www.liveart.ai/api)).
- **Consumer "antique identifier" apps** have no independent tests. Their own guides say identification beats pricing ([Antique Identifier](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/ai-antique-appraisal-accuracy/)).
- **The gap:** no vendor found publishes measured value accuracy against realized prices. Zillow does publish its error: a 1.83% median error on homes listed for sale and 7.01% on homes that are not ([Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/zestimate)). That is the model to copy.

**What appraisers say about AI.**
- **USPAP.** The Appraisal Standards Board adopted Advisory Opinion 41 on 23 April 2026, replacing AO-18 (AVMs) and AO-37 (computer-assisted valuation tools).
  - The appraiser stays responsible for the scope of work, for whether a tool is fit for the job, for the reliability of its inputs and outputs, and for confidentiality.
  - Using a tool alone triggers no disclosure, unless leaving it out would mislead ([ISA on AO-41](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/550/artificial-intelligence-and-uspap-understanding-advisory-opinion-41); [ISA on disclosure](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/552)).
- **ASA's statement** (effective 28 January 2026) recommends naming AI where it was relied on for research or analysis, and offers a sample sentence ([ASA](https://www.appraisers.org/about/standards-ethics-and-policies/standards/artificial-intelligence)).
- **ISA's "AI & Appraising: Proceed with Caution"** (19 August 2026) says AI "cannot replace" judgment and its conclusions need supporting evidence ([ISA](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/about/blog/details/556)).
- **ASA's course PP167** trains appraisers to spot "common error patterns in chatbot 'appraisals', including object ID, dating, and comparables" ([ASA PP167](https://learn.appraisers.org/products/pp167-artificial-intelligence-ai-tools-and-trends-for-the-uspap-compliant-appraiser-2)). These are exactly the three places Havellin's agents must be strongest.
- **The academic picture.** A machine-learning model trained on images and catalogue data added information beyond the auction houses' own estimates. The experts still had condition and provenance, which the model lacked ([Aubry et al., *Journal of Finance* 2023](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/manso/papers/biased-auctioneers.html)).

## D. Identifying and valuing: failure modes, techniques and measurement

### D.1 Where vision-language models fail

- **Authenticity.** On CArtBench, telling a genuine piece from a close confound "stays near chance" for nine models ([arXiv 2604.11632](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11632)).
- **Period and dating.** On a temporal anachronism benchmark of 1,600 artifacts, the best model scored 58.7% ([arXiv 2605.15071](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15071v1)). Predictions of culture, period and creator were "inconsistent and weakly grounded" ([Appear2Meaning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07338)).
- **Fine-grained look-alikes.** Leading vision-language models scored "unsatisfactory" on fine-grained retail classes, and a CLIP plus DINOv2 ensemble beat them ([MIMEX](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2409.14963)).
- **Marks.** A 925 stamp struck into base metal is reported as sterling. A photograph cannot test the metal ([Goldealers](https://goldealers.co.uk/post/014-ai-hallmark-identifier-launch)) (vendor). Restorations, married pieces and later monograms mislead ([Antique Identifier](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/ai-antique-appraisal-accuracy/)) (vendor).
- **Counting.** The top model reached 42.9% on cluttered real scenes, and error grows with the count ([CountQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06585v1); [arXiv 2511.17722](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17722)).
- **Citations.** AI search tools gave the wrong headline, publisher, date or link on more than 60% of 1,600 queries, "with alarming confidence" ([Columbia Tow Center via Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/03/18/ai-search-engines-confidently-wrong-citing-sources-columbia-study)). Museum-metadata pilots found invented artworks ([arXiv 2506.19813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19813)).
- **Anchoring.** Models are pulled toward a number in the prompt. Stronger models are pulled more consistently, and chain-of-thought or reflection prompts do not fix it ([arXiv 2505.15392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15392); [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.16/)).
- **Overconfidence.** Stated confidence is poorly calibrated: GPT-4 told its right answers from its wrong ones at an AUROC of 62.7% ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)). LLM house-price intervals were overconfident ([Geerts et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11812)).

### D.2 What improves accuracy, with the evidence

1. **More of the right pixels.**
   - Close-ups of marks, labels, backs and undersides; text that is legible without losing context; images before text ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)).
   - Claude reads 28-pixel patches, so a 1,200 × 1,600 close-up costs about 2,500 tokens (derived from the same page).
   - Size drives value for paintings, rugs and furniture, and Rev. Proc. 96-15 requires it for paintings. A tape in the frame, or a size said aloud, is cheap.
2. **Retrieval before naming.**
   - Retrieving candidates, then letting the model rank them, raised fine-grained recognition on five benchmarks ([RAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13805)).
   - Region-level retrieval grounded better than whole-image retrieval ([PixSearch](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19060)).
   - For patterns and marks the references exist: Replacements' identification tool, WorthPoint's marks library, and above all Havellin's own lines once an appraiser has confirmed them.
3. **Similar sold examples in front of the model.** Choosing in-context examples by similarity "significantly enhance[s]" LLM appraisal ([Geerts et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11812)). This is what research-depth valuation already does with comparables, and it argues for feeding Havellin's own realized lines back in.
4. **Several independent answers.**
   - Self-consistency, sampling several reasoning paths and aggregating them, added 4 to 18 points on reasoning benchmarks ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171v4)).
   - An ensemble of 12 LLMs forecast as well as 925 humans, and averaging human with machine did better still ([Schoenegger et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19379v6)).
   - For a price, take the median of two or three blind valuations, and treat disagreement as "unsure".
5. **Evidence first, and no anchors.**
   - Ask for the comparable, then the figure.
   - Keep every prior number out of the valuing prompt (a walkthrough ballpark, an earlier figure, a typed estimate), since prompting does not undo anchoring.
   - Havellin's `2026-10-26b` rule, that a comparable must be one item's own page with its price, is the right guard against the citation failure above.
6. **Abstain where the model is at chance.** Prefer "conservative attribution with explicit abstention when evidence is weak" ([Surrey, arXiv 2603.11147](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11147v1)). Never let a model attest authenticity. Route signed art, designer jewelry, watches, Asian ceramics and named makers to an appraiser whatever the figure.
7. **Measured confidence, not stated confidence.**
   - Map each confidence word to its observed hit rate on Havellin's own data.
   - Build ranges with split conformal prediction, which guarantees coverage "regardless of whether the model is correct". The guarantee is marginal, so calibrate by category ([theorempath](https://theorempath.com/topics/split-conformal-prediction); [Diamant et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/diamant24a/diamant24a.pdf)).
8. **A floor from physics.** Weight × purity × spot price gives a melt value that no comparable can undercut.

### D.3 How to measure accuracy

- **Ground truth.**
  - Havellin's own realized prices, from proceeds statements per line, net and gross.
  - Appraisers' figures on the lines they value.
  - A seeded set of 200 to 300 public realized lots from after the model's training cutoff, with the item's own sale page excluded from search so the model cannot find the answer. Frozen models carry pre-cutoff prices ([arXiv 2604.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21433)).
- **Metrics, reported by category and by value band.**
  - **Median absolute percentage error** and **hit rates within ±25% and ±50%**. Zillow reports both kinds ([Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/zestimate)).
  - **Median ratio** of predicted to realized (target 1.0; property assessors accept 0.90 to 1.10).
  - **Coefficient of dispersion** (property assessors accept 15% or less on homes).
  - **Price-related differential** (0.98 to 1.03; above 1.03 means cheap lots are overvalued and dear ones undervalued) ([IAAO via Philadelphia OPA](https://www.phila.gov/media/20220715130400/OPA-Tax-Year-2023-Ratio-Study.pdf)). This bias is documented in human auction estimates too ([Indian art study](https://scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2022/113172/pdf/index.html); [Ashenfelter & Graddy via NBER](https://www.nber.org/digest/oct02/art-good-investment)).
  - **Interval coverage**: an 80% range should hold the realized price 80% of the time.
  - **Calibration by confidence level.**
  - **Identification accuracy** on maker, pattern, period and material, as judged by the appraiser.
  - **Abstention rate.**
- **Human benchmarks.**
  - In 2016 and 2017, Sotheby's lots sold within or above estimate 80% of the time and Christie's 74% ([Mearto](https://mearto.com/accuracy-of-sothebys-and-christies-estimations-revealed)).
  - Television verbal values are "approximations" on which experts vary ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/roadshow/appraisals/china-trade-davenport-ca-1850/)).
- **Published LLM valuation accuracy.**
  - Price accuracy rises with model size ([LLP, arXiv 2510.09347](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09347)).
  - A fine-tuned 7B model reached 16.86% mean absolute percentage error on Amazon products, against 42.68% for the baselines ([arXiv 2506.06657](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06657)).
  - LLMs approach machine-learning models on house prices ([Geerts et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11812)).
  - **No published study was found on LLM valuation of antiques or second-hand household goods from photographs.** Havellin's own measured figure would be the first in its market.

## E. What best in class would mean, ranked

Ranked by accuracy gained per dollar and per concierge minute. Costs are rough. Anthropic's search price is published; token costs depend on the model's list price, so check the pricing page.

1. **Measure against realized prices, and publish it.**
   - Join each valued line to its realized gross from the proceeds statements.
   - Add a 200 to 300-lot time-split golden set.
   - Report, per category and value band, the median error, the ±25% and ±50% hit rates, the median ratio, the price-related differential and the interval coverage. Rerun at every prompt or model change.
   - Give trust officers a one-page accuracy statement, as Zillow does.
   - *Evidence:* no competitor or tool publishes one (B); AO-41 makes the human responsible for knowing a tool's reliability.
   - *Cost:* a few days to build; the golden-set run is about 300 research calls, roughly 1,500 searches or $15 in search fees plus tokens.
2. **Blind, double valuation on researched lines.**
   - Two independent research runs (two models, or two prompts with different searches).
   - Take the median; a spread beyond a set ratio reads *unsure*.
   - No prior figure in the prompt.
   - *Evidence:* self-consistency and ensemble gains; anchoring (D.1).
   - *Cost:* about twice the research spend on perhaps 10% to 20% of lines.
3. **Ranges that mean what they say.**
   - Replace stated confidence with an empirical, conformal 80% range for each category, from item 1's data.
   - Print the range basis beside the figure.
   - *Evidence:* Xiong; Geerts; split conformal coverage.
   - *Cost:* small, once item 1 exists.
4. **Label every comparable's price basis and normalise it.**
   - Hammer, hammer plus premium, asking or retail, and the sale date.
   - An estate's fair market value is the auction market including the premium (LTR 9235005; ISA); an asking or retail figure is a ceiling with a discount, never the value.
   - *Evidence:* the bases differ by source (C).
   - *Cost:* one schema field and prompt lines.
5. **Licensed sold data where the open web is thin.**
   - Apply for eBay Marketplace Insights.
   - Approach Liquidity Services (Invaluable) and ATG (LiveAuctioneers) for an internal-use licence.
   - For art, take Artnet's business package or LiveArt's API.
   - Buy WatchCharts and Greysheet only if watches or coins recur.
   - *Evidence:* the best realized data sits behind logins or terms (C).
   - *Cost:* from about $100 a month (Greysheet), or $200 per 2,500 WatchCharts credits, or $1,175 a year (an Artnet appraiser seat), up to bespoke feeds.
6. **Abstain on authenticity and route it.**
   - A flag for "value depends on authenticity" (signed art, designer jewelry, watches, Asian ceramics, Tiffany-type makers) that sends the line to an appraiser whatever the figure.
   - The agents name and estimate, and never attest.
   - *Evidence:* CArtBench is at chance on authenticity; ASA's PP167 names object ID and dating as chatbot failure points.
   - *Cost:* nil.
7. **A reference library from Havellin's own confirmed lines.**
   - Every line an appraiser confirms (photograph, mark close-up, identification, realized price) becomes a reference retrieved for the next job's look-alikes.
   - Replacements' identification tool covers unknown patterns by hand.
   - *Evidence:* RAR and PixSearch; MIMEX on look-alikes.
   - *Cost:* moderate engineering. The data accrues free with every job.
8. **Physical floors and the few measurements that move value.**
   - Weigh sterling and gold lots once per batch and value them at melt as a floor.
   - Say the size of paintings, rugs and case pieces at the camera (Agent One already writes a stated size into the name).
   - *Evidence:* the counterfeit-hallmark finding; Rev. Proc. 96-15.
   - *Cost:* a kitchen scale, a free spot-price API, and seconds per batch, which fits "thorough, never cumbersome".
9. **A defensible paper.** Room by room under §20.2031-6, a basis, comparables and a valuation date on each line, appraisers' reports on their lines: mostly built already. What is missing is an AI disclosure sentence (ASA's statement; for counsel's bundle) and the printed accuracy figure from item 1. *Evidence:* §733.604(3); *Kollsman*. *Cost:* small.

**Not confirmed, and worth a call:** whether WorthPoint, Invaluable or LiveAuctioneers license data at all; Invaluable's premium basis; Artnet's premium basis; current WorthPoint and Invaluable prices; the size of the Sotheby's and Christie's presence in Palm Beach; and local appraisers' fees and turnaround. No source found measures any competitor's or tool's valuation accuracy.
