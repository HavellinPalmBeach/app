Photographing a House

How to photograph a house so the inventory comes out right the first time. Written for beginners.

For Anyone photographing a house for an inventory Camera The Havellin app: Job Plan, then the room Status Draft for Anthony Issued 6 Oct 2026

How it works

Your photos become the inventory: the list of everything in the house that the family, the attorney and the appraiser will rely on. The app looks at each photo on its own and writes down everything it can see in it. It cannot tell that two photos show the same thing, so if you photograph a vase twice, the list says there are two vases.

Rule 1

Photograph each thing once

One item photo for each thing, or for each group of everyday things. Never a second item photo of the same thing.

Rule 2

Extra angles are close-ups

For the back, the signature or a closer look, press Close-up, the round button beside the shutter, once for each picture. A close-up is filed with the thing you just photographed and never adds a new item to the list.

Rule 3

Different destination, different photo

The destination you tap and the Appraise flag stick to one thing per photo. Anything else in that photo comes back with no destination and no Appraise flag. So anything going somewhere different, or needing an appraisal, gets its own photo.

The camera’s four buttons

ButtonUse it forOn the list?
📷 As found The room exactly as you found it, before anything is moved. No. A record of the room; it cannot be deleted.
📷 Items One photo of each thing, or of each group of everyday things. Yes. Each thing in it goes on the list.
Close-up Close-ups of the thing you just photographed: the back, a signature, a label, damage. One press each. No. Filed with that thing.
📷 After The room once it is empty. No.

The buttons along the bottom say where the next thing is going: Keep, New home (when the client is moving), To a person, Donate, Sell, Remove or Undecided (the default). Your choice stays selected until you change it. ⚑ Appraise marks the next photo only.

Example: a vase on a dresser

Wrong
  1. Items: the dresser, with the vase on it.
  2. Items: the vase, up close.

The list says: dresser, vase, vase. One vase, counted twice.

Right
  1. Tap the vase’s destination, then Items: the vase on its own, filling the photo.
  2. Hold to talk: “Mom’s, from Venice, going to Karen.”
  3. Close-up: the mark on its base.
  4. Put the vase down somewhere else.
  5. Items: the dresser, cleared off.

The list says: vase, dresser. Each with its own photo, destination and note.

If you would mind seeing it in a box of mixed things, give it its own photo.

One room, step by step

Read the room’s notes

  • The box at the top of the room’s screen lists what to look for, any firearms, cash or safes, notes from the walkthrough (the first visit, for the estimate), and any collection noted then that is still waiting for its photo.
  • One person photographs a room. If two of you are working, take different rooms.

As-found photos

  • From the doorway, then each wall, then inside every drawer, cabinet, closet and box. Move nothing and tidy nothing first.
  • On an estate, take the as-found photos of the whole house before anyone sorts anything.
  • On an estate, then tick As-found pass complete. In a client’s own home the photos are enough.
  • Next: Items, in the camera, goes straight on to the item photos of the same room.

Sort

  • Pull out anything that needs its own photo (the list below).
  • Gather everyday things of one kind, going to the same place, onto one surface: the paperbacks, the linens, the everyday glasses.
  • Keep pairs and sets together.

Item photos

  • Work around the room in one direction. The things you pulled out first, one photo each, then each group.
  • Furniture last: clear it, close the drawers, then photograph it.
  • Before every photo, check the edges. Nothing you have photographed, or will photograph, should be in it.
  • A full bookcase or china cabinet you cannot empty: take out anything special first, then photograph the rest once, as it stands.

Notes and close-ups

  • Talk first. Straight after the item photo, hold to talk: who it is going to, family history, “set of 8”, the size of a painting or rug. A note said after a close-up goes on the thing the close-up is of, so it is read with the item.
  • Then the close-ups: press Close-up for each one. Most important first: the app only reads the first four.
  • Close-ups cannot be added later, so take them before the next item.

Before you leave the room

  • Nothing should say ⚠ not saved. If it does, tap Retry.
  • Tap Lock to mark the room as photographed. Once it has been emptied, take an After photo and tap Cleared.
  • Finish a room in one go. A room photographed over two days is where doubles come from.

Its own photo, or a group photo?

Its own photo

  • Art & Décor, Antiques, Jewelry & Watches, Silver & Precious Metal, Rugs & Carpets, Collectibles, Firearms, Wine & Spirits and Musical Instruments. The app lists each of these separately anyway.
  • Anything signed, stamped or hallmarked.
  • Anything that may need an appraisal.
  • Anything going to a particular person, or somewhere different from what is around it.
  • On an estate filing a federal estate tax return (Form 706): anything that might be worth more than $100. Tax rules do not allow those inside a group.

A group photo

  • Everyday things of one kind going to the same place: books, linens, kitchenware, everyday glasses and dishes, tools, clothes on a rail, holiday decorations.
  • Spread them out so they can be counted. The app lists them as one item with a count, like “Flatware, 30 pieces”.
  • A pair or a set goes in one photo, together.

Special situations

A collection from the walkthrough
It is already on the inventory, waiting for its photo. When you find it, tap its name in the room’s notes (or in the camera), then take the photo: the photo goes onto that collection. Photographed it as an ordinary item by mistake? That gets fixed on the computer (below).
A set split between rooms
Bring it together and photograph it once. If it cannot move, photograph the main group, say “set of 8, two more in the study”, and keep the other two out of the study’s photos.
A rug under furniture
Photograph it after the furniture on it, close on the pattern and a corner, and say “rug only”.
Mirrors and glass doors
Shoot at an angle so nothing else, and nobody, shows in the reflection.
Cars and boats
No item photo. They are added on the computer from the estimate. Keep them out of the garage’s photos.
Papers and wills
Photograph the box or stack from the side, never the pages. A will, deed or title is set aside unread and handed over against a signed receipt; an original will goes to the estate attorney the same day (Concierge Guide, Step 10g).
Cash
No item photo. On an estate, two people count it, seal it and record it on the Found on site card (Concierge Guide, Step 10g).
Medicines
As-found photos only. Never the labels.
People
Keep everyone out of every photo, yourself in a mirror included. Framed family photos are fine as a group.
Firearms
One item photo each, where it lies. Do not touch it. Everything after that follows the Firearms Protocol.

Taking a good photo

  • Lights on, blinds open, and never aim at a window. The camera has no flash.
  • Fill the photo with the thing: step back for big pieces, in for small ones.
  • Hold still for a second so the picture sharpens.
  • Glass, framed art and silver: step to one side to lose the glare.
  • Put small things on something plain, like a towel or the bed.
  • Blurry? Tap the trash can on the Last panel straight away and take it again.

After the visit, on the computer

On the Job Admin & Inv tab, in this order.

  1. Tap Retry on anything not saved, then Name N shots. The app names every photo, then compares all the photos of each room to find anything photographed twice. Check N rooms runs that comparison again.
  2. Possible duplicates (at the top): look at both photos, then tap Remove on the extra one, or Not duplicates if they really are two things.
  3. An item marked 📷 no photograph yet is a walkthrough collection still waiting for its photo. If it was photographed as an ordinary item, open it, pick that photo and tap Use that line.
  4. An item marked 🔗 1 of N in this photo came out of a photo of several things. Give it its destination, and move the Appraise flag to the right item.
  5. Compare each room’s list with its as-found photos: anything missing, anything doubled?
  6. Then tap Value N lines.

What the app cannot catch yet

  • Something photographed in two different rooms: the comparison only looks within one room.
  • In a big room the app compares photos in overlapping batches of 12. Two photos taken within six of each other are always compared; further apart, a double can be missed.

Shooting a room

  1. As found first. Nothing moved.
  2. One item photo per thing. Check the edges.
  3. Extra angles: press Close-up.
  4. Different destination, different photo.
  5. Special things out first, one photo each.
  6. Small things first, furniture last.
  7. Talk before the close-ups.
  8. Blurry? Delete it and take it again.
  9. One person per room. Finish the room.
  10. Walkthrough collection: tap it, then shoot it.
