Photographing a House

How to shoot a house so the inventory comes out right the first time: every object counted once, named from its own picture, and carrying the close-ups that let it be valued.

For Every job that keeps an inventory Camera The room’s camera, on the Job Plan Status Draft for Anthony Issued 6 Oct 2026
Rule 01

One thing, one Items shot

Every object is the subject of exactly one Items shot. The agent reads each Items shot on its own, with no memory of the one before, and writes a line for everything it can see in it, so two shots of the vase are two vase lines. The room check catches most of them at the desk; each one it catches is still a line somebody has to remove.

Rule 02

Every other picture is a Detail

The zoom, the back, the mark, the label, the damage: tap + Detail of last before each one, straight after the item’s own shot. A Detail rides with its item and never becomes a line. Miss the tap and it is a second Items shot.

Rule 03

One frame, one fate

The chip you tapped and ⚑ Appraise land on one line per picture. When the agent finds several things in a frame, the others come back Undecided and unflagged. Anything going somewhere else, or worth a flag or a word of its own, gets its own shot.

How the app reads your pictures

Four kinds of shot. Only one of them makes inventory lines.

ShotWhat it becomesHow many
📷 As found The record of the room before anything moved, printed on the As-Found Record. Never a line, never read by the agent, and it cannot be deleted. As many as you like.
📷 Items Inventory lines. Agent One writes a line for every thing it can see: ordinary household goods as one line with a count, and a line each for Art & Décor, Antiques, Jewelry & Watches, Silver & Precious Metal, Rugs & Carpets, Collectibles, Firearms, Wine & Spirits, Musical Instruments, and anything marked, signed or plainly valuable. One per object or lot, ever.
+ Detail of last A close-up of the last Items shot’s object. Both agents read up to four with their item, and the desk shows them under it. Never a line. As many as the object earns.
📷 After The room once it is empty. Never a line. One or two, before Cleared.

What that means in the room

  • The agent sees one Items shot at a time: the picture, its close-ups, the room’s name and your note. It cannot know it has already seen the vase.
  • Everything in the frame counts, the background too. The lamp at the edge of the dresser shot becomes a lamp line, and a second one when you shoot the side table.
  • The app keeps a 900-pixel copy of each shot. A mark or a signature has to fill a close-up to be read.
  • At the desk, once the shots are named, the room check reads each room’s photographs together and flags any line that is one thing photographed twice, whatever the two lines are called (“Blue vase” and “cobalt art glass vase, likely Murano”). It flags; a person removes the extra line. It is the net, not the method: shoot as if it were not there.

The blue vase on the dresser

The pattern for every surface in the house.

Wrong

Two Items shots

  1. Items: the dresser, vase on top.
  2. Items: the vase, zoomed in.

Dresser, blue vase, blue vase. Two vases. The room check should flag the pair at the desk, and somebody removes one: a tap that did not need to happen.

Quick

One shot and its Details

  1. Items: the dresser, vase on top.
  2. + Detail of last: the vase, close.
  3. + Detail of last: the mark on its base.

Dresser and blue vase, one each. The vase line shares the dresser’s picture, comes back Undecided with no ⚑ whatever you tapped, and its close-ups sit under the dresser’s line at the desk. Fine for an ordinary vase.

Best

The vase gets its own frame

  1. Items: the vase alone, filling the frame, with the chip for where it is going (and ⚑ Appraise if it deserves one).
  2. Hold to talk: “Mom’s, from Venice, promised to Karen.”
  3. + Detail of last: the mark on its base.
  4. Set the vase down off the dresser (you are holding it anyway).
  5. Items: the dresser, bare.
  6. + Detail of last: the maker’s label in the top drawer.

Two lines, each with its own picture, chip, flag, close-up and note. Nothing to fix at the desk.

The test: if you would mind seeing it in a box lot, it gets its own frame.

A room, start to finish

The order is what keeps a thing in one frame. Same order, every room.

Before the camera opens

In the doorway

  • Read the brief at the top of the room: the must-finds, firearms, cash and safes, the walkthrough note.
  • Check the brief for From the walkthrough, not photographed yet: the collections the walkthrough recorded. Each is on the inventory already, waiting for its photograph.
  • One shooter per room. Two of you on the job: take different rooms.

As found

Before anything moves; on an estate, the whole house first

  • From the door, then each wall, then every drawer, cabinet, closet and box opened and shot as it sits.
  • Nothing moves and nothing is tidied. These shots never become lines and cannot be deleted, so shoot freely.
  • On an estate, do every room before anyone sorts anything, so no room is touched before its record exists.
  • Tick As-found pass complete in each room.

Sort

Before the Items pass

  • Pull out what gets its own frame (the list below).
  • Gather everyday things of one kind and one fate on one surface: the paperbacks, the linens, the everyday glasses. A shelf that is already one lot stays where it is.
  • Pairs and sets go together: both lamps, all eight chairs, the whole dinner service.

Items

Round the room in one direction from the door

  • Pulled-out pieces first, one frame each, filling it. Then each lot, with its chip.
  • Each piece of furniture top to bottom: what sits on it, what is in it, then the piece itself, bare, drawers shut.
  • Drawers: the contents as a lot, framed tight on the contents, then shut the drawer.
  • Before every shot, look at the edges. Anything with its own shot, taken or to come, is in the way: move it or step in.
  • What you cannot empty (a full bookcase, a china cabinet you will not unload): shoot it once, full, and the agent splits it. Pull out anything that needs its own frame first, and never shoot those contents again.
  • Big pieces are shot where they stand.
  • A walkthrough collection: tap it on the room’s brief (or its chip in the camera) and shoot it. The photograph goes on its line, which keeps its number. Close-ups after it, as for anything else.

Close-ups and words

Straight after each item’s own shot, before the next item

  • Talk first. Hold to talk (or type): who it is going to, family history, “pair”, “set of 8”, the size of a painting or a rug. The note rides the last shot, and a note said over a close-up stays on the close-up, where neither the agent nor the desk reads it.
  • Then + Detail of last, one tap per close-up: it resets after every shot. The mark or the signature first: the agents read up to four.
  • Close-ups cannot be added later. + Detail of last only reaches the last Items shot since the camera opened.
  • Blurry? Press the 🗑 on the Last panel at once, then reshoot. A saved close-up has no tile in the room, so the camera is the only place to bin it.

Before you leave the room

Then Lock

  • Open the room’s strip: no ⚠ not saved (press Retry), +1 detail on every item you took close-ups of, nothing shot twice.
  • Finish the room before you leave it. A room shot over two days is where doubles come from; the room check reads both days together at the desk, but each double it finds is still a line to remove.
  • Lock when it is sorted and shot. 📷 After once it is empty, then Cleared.

Its own frame, or a shared one

Decide while you sort, not while you shoot.

Its own frame, filling it

  • Art & Décor, Antiques, Jewelry & Watches, Silver & Precious Metal, Rugs & Carpets, Collectibles, Firearms, Wine & Spirits, Musical Instruments. The agent gives each a line anyway; give each its own picture.
  • Anything signed, stamped or hallmarked.
  • Anything you would tap ⚑ Appraise for.
  • Anything going to a named person, or somewhere different from what is around it.
  • A must-find. Tap Found it on the brief as well.
  • On an estate filing a Form 706: anything that might be worth more than $100. No article in a lot may be (Treas. Reg. §20.2031-6(a)).
  • Anything you would mind seeing in a box lot.

A shared frame, one lot, one chip

  • Ordinary things of one kind with one fate: books, linens, kitchenware, everyday glass and china, tools, garage and cleaning supplies, clothing on a rail, holiday decorations.
  • Spread them so they can be counted. The agent writes “Flatware, 30 pieces” as one line with a count.
  • A pair or a set: in one frame, together.

Close-ups worth taking

Each one is a + Detail of last, taken straight after the object’s own shot. They turn “appears to be” into a reading, and they are what Agent Two values from.

Tricky spots

A set split across rooms
Bring it together and shoot it once. If it cannot move, shoot the main group, say “set of 8, two more in the study”, and keep the two out of every Items frame in the study.
A rug under furniture
Shoot it after the furniture on it, tight on the pattern and a corner, and say “rug only”. The agent reads your words with the picture. That helps; it is not a guarantee, so check it at the desk.
Mirrors and glass doors
A reflection puts other things in the frame. Shoot at an angle, and keep yourself out of it.
A collection on the walkthrough
It is on the inventory already, as a line reading no photograph yet, and the Job Plan says so until it is photographed. When you find it, tap it on the room’s brief (or its chip in the camera) and shoot it: the photograph goes on that line. Shot it as an ordinary Items line by mistake? At the desk, open the collection’s line and pick the line you shot; the collection is counted once.
Vehicles
Add them from the desk’s From the Estimate Walkthrough panel: it carries the year, the description and the condition. Keep them out of the garage’s Items frames.
Papers
Shoot the box, the drawer or the stack from the side, never the pages. A will, codicil, deed or title is set aside unread and handed over against a signed receipt, an original will to the estate attorney the same day (the playbook, Step 10g). Never photograph its pages.
Cash
Not an Items shot. Two of you count it and seal it, on the Found on site card (Step 10g), and the desk adds it as a line of its own.
Medicines
As found only, never the labels.
People
Keep them out of every frame: the family, the crew, and yourself in a mirror. Framed family photographs are fine as a lot.
Firearms
One Items shot each, where it lies, untouched. Everything after that is the Firearms Protocol.

Shooting well

  • Lights on and blinds open, and never into a window. The camera in the app has no flash.
  • Fill the frame with the subject. Step back for big pieces, in for small ones.
  • Hold level and still for a beat, until the picture sharpens.
  • Glass, framed art and silver: a step to the side kills the glare.
  • Small things on something plain: a towel, the bed.

Never

  1. Never shoot one thing on two Items shots.
  2. Never take a close-up without tapping + Detail of last first.
  3. Never leave a bad shot in place. Bin it from Last and reshoot.
  4. Never two people on one room’s Items pass.
  5. Never shoot a walkthrough collection without tapping it first.
  6. Never photograph the inside of private papers or a will, a medicine label, or a person.
  7. Never move or handle a firearm for a picture beyond what the Firearms Protocol allows.

At the desk that evening

On the Job Admin & Inv tab, in this order.

  1. Retry anything ⚠ not saved, then press Name N shots. When the naming finishes, the room check runs by itself; Check N rooms runs it again for any room with new lines.
  2. Possible duplicates, above the rows: the same name twice in a room, or what the room check saw. Open both pictures, then press Remove on the extra line or Not duplicates.
  3. A line reading 📷 no photograph yet is a walkthrough collection still waiting for its photograph: take it in the house, or pick the line already shot of it in its panel.
  4. Rows reading 🔗 1 of N in this photo: the agent split that picture. Set the disposition on the lines it split off (they come back Undecided), move Needs Appraisal to the right line in its panel, and check each name says which object it is, because every line off that picture shows the whole picture.
  5. Read each room’s lines against its as-found shots: anything doubled, anything missing.
  6. Work the agent · unsure rows first.
  7. Then Value N lines. Agent Two values from the same picture and close-ups, so a clean frame is a better figure.

Where the app still leans on you

What the method above works around. Each is on the open list for after the pilot.

  • Lines split off one picture come back Undecided and unflagged, and the desk shows the picture’s close-ups under its first line only.
  • A note said over a close-up stays on the close-up: neither agent nor desk reads it.
  • The room check looks within one room. A thing moved and shot in two rooms is not caught.
  • A room of more than 12 photographs is read in overlapping runs of 12, in the order taken: two shots of one thing within six of each other are always read together, and further apart they can miss each other.

Field card · print and keep it in the kit

Shooting a room

  1. As found first. Whole house, nothing moved. Never lines.
  2. One thing, one Items shot. Check the edges.
  3. Every other picture: tap + Detail of last first.
  4. One frame, one fate.
  5. Pull out what matters. Its own frame, filling it.
  6. Smalls first, furniture last.
  7. Talk before the close-ups.
  8. Bad shot? 🗑 now, then reshoot.
  9. One shooter per room. Finish the room.
  10. Walkthrough collections: tap it, then shoot it.
