Packing a House

How to pack so that unpacking is putting boxes where they belong, not opening every one to find out what is inside.

For The concierge and the crew packing a home In the app Job Plan, Midpoint & pickups, under the boxes Status Draft for Anthony Issued 17 Oct 2026

Set up before the first box

  • A packing zone out of the sorting path. Sorting and packing in the same room is how a Keep gets packed with the donations.
  • Storage and nonessentials first, the rooms the client lives in last. The kitchen they cook in and the bathroom they use are packed the day before the move, not the week before.
  • Only what is Keep or New home gets packed. Sale, donation and disposal leave the house in their own pass. If a thing has no destination tapped yet, it is not ready to pack.
Rule 1

Label the sides

Never the top: tops are hidden when boxes are stacked. On two sides of every box: the room it goes to, what is in it, and FRAGILE or HEAVY where it applies.

Rule 2

A few boxes say OPEN FIRST

Bedding, towels, the coffee maker, the kettle, basic cookware, toilet paper, light bulbs, a tool kit. These are opened in the first hour at the new home.

Rule 3

Hardware goes in bags

Every screw and bracket from a piece of furniture goes in a labelled bag with the piece's name on it. Every bag goes in one parts box, and the parts box stays with the concierge, never on the truck.

Fragile and high-value pieces

  • Each wrapped and padded on its own; extra protection for glass, china and heirlooms; marked so the crew loads and unloads it with care. Artwork and mirrors are crated.
  • High-value pieces are kept apart and counted on and off the truck. The Appraise flag you pressed in the house tells you which they are.
  • Jewelry, cash and the irreplaceable never go on the truck. They travel with the client or a trusted family member. Say so on the pre-job call so they are set aside before the crew arrives.
  • A box inventory, kept with the job records: the box count per room and what the fragile ones hold. The mover's own inventory is signed on move day and filed from the Job Plan.

The DO NOT LOAD closet

Pick one closet or small room, put a sign on the door, and nothing in it gets loaded. No exceptions. It holds:

  • The to-go bag (Move Day lists what goes in it): medications, documents, chargers, a change of clothes, comfort items.
  • The documents the client will need in the first week, and anything valuable that is travelling with the family.
  • The keys and codes for the new home, and the parts box.
  • Anything going to a person who is collecting it from the house.

The day before

  • Fridge and freezer emptied, unplugged and defrosted at least 24 hours before the truck; the door propped open.
  • Gas-powered tools drained and left to dry. Propane tanks disconnected; they do not travel.
  • Anything that needs a technician to disconnect (a gas range, a wall-mounted television, a plumbed-in refrigerator) is booked before move day, not discovered on it.

Never packed

  • Hazardous materials: paint, solvents, pool chemicals, fuel, fertiliser, aerosols, ammunition. Disposed of under the local rules, never boxed. Firearms have their own protocol.
  • Houseplants on a long-distance move. Rehome them; they rarely survive the truck.
  • Perishable food and anything open and liquid.

What the mover covers

When the mover is chosen, ask what the load is insured for. If the client thinks their things are worth more than that, have the mover quote the extra coverage before the date is fixed. The new building or community insures nothing of a resident's. The Before Day 1 stage carries this under Choosing the mover.

Packing

  1. Zone out of the sorting path.
  2. Storage first, daily rooms last.
  3. Label two sides: room, contents, FRAGILE or HEAVY.
  4. A few OPEN FIRST boxes.
  5. Hardware bagged, one parts box, with the concierge.
  6. Fragile wrapped alone, art crated.
  7. Jewelry and valuables with the family.
  8. DO NOT LOAD closet, sign on the door.
  9. Fridge off 24 hours before. Propane off.
  10. No paint, fuel or chemicals on the truck.
