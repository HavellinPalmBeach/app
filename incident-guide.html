Reporting an Incident

Anything that goes wrong or needs attention on a job is recorded the same day, with photographs, in enough detail to answer the client, their attorney and the insurer. The person first, then the house, then the record.

For Anyone on a Havellin job, on the phone in the house In the app Job Plan, In the house, the Incidents card Status Draft for Anthony Issued 19 Oct 2026

The first ten minutes

  • Somebody hurt: help them first. Call 911 if there is any doubt. Nothing in the app comes before that.
  • A hazard (mold, a weak floor, chemicals, a gas smell, an animal, a live wire): everyone out of that area, and work stops there until Anthony says otherwise.
  • An injury, a missing item or a worry about an older person in the house: call Anthony at once, (617) 650-6588. The app will turn red and say the same; the call comes first.
  • Then record it, today, in the house, before the details fade. A report takes about a minute.
Rule 1

Record it today

On the day it happens, or as soon after as you can, with the day it happened on it. Memory fades and photographs get taken over; a record written the same day is what a claim is paid on.

Rule 2

Nothing is edited or deleted

Once recorded, the report stays as written. A correction, a photograph, the resolution: each is a new entry with your name and the time. One recorded in error is voided with a reason and stays on the record, struck through.

Rule 3

Facts, not fault

Write what you saw, in order, in plain words. No blame, no apology, no promise of payment and no guess at who was responsible. The printed record says so itself: it is not an admission of liability or a statement of value.

Seven kinds. Each asks the same things (when, where, what happened, who was present, who reported it, photographs) and one or two questions of its own.

Recording it

Press Report an incident

The Incidents card · Job Plan, In the house

  • The card sits under the in-house boxes on every job (beside Found on site on an estate; the Home Prep plan has it too). Tap the kind.

When and where

The real day, the time, the place

  • Today is filled in. Put the day it happened and the time; the day is never after today.
  • Where: the room, the garage, the driveway, the yard, the truck, the new home.

What happened

In order, as you saw it

  • What was being done, what happened, what was done next. Quote what people said, in their words.
  • Leave out what you think went wrong and whose fault it was. That is for Anthony and the carrier.

Who was present, who reported it

Names

  • Names are offered as you type; a semicolon between them. Reported by is you, unless somebody else saw it and told you.

The kind’s own question

One or two, no more

  • Who was hurt; the item and a rough value; where it was last seen; whether work stopped. A value is an estimate: say so in the account if it is a guess.

Photographs

Wide, close, scale

  • Wide first, so a reader knows where it was. Then close: the damage, the item, the hazard. Then something for scale beside it.
  • Both sides of a broken thing, and the box or wrapping it was in.
  • On damage, photograph it where it lies before anything is moved.

Press Record

The report is written

  • The photographs follow it to the client’s Incident Reports folder in Drive. One that did not get there is named on the card: press Add photos and send it again.
  • The app refuses only what it cannot record without: the kind, the day, where, what, who reported it and the kind’s own question. Everything else can wait for a note.

Property damage: the six promises

The estate agreement promises the client six things when we damage something (its §6.3), and the card holds us to them on every job, estate or living. Each reminder on the card turns red once its day has passed.

The promiseIn the app
Photograph and describe it at onceThe report, with its photographs.
Tell the client within 24 hoursPress Client notified once they have been told, with who and how.
Leave it where it is: nothing moved, repaired or thrown away without the client’s sayNothing to press. The photographs show it in place.

Say you are sorry it happened. Never say we will pay for it: the carrier decides that, and the agreement sets the limits.

The call to Anthony

  • An injury, a missing item or a vulnerable adult concern turns the top of the Job Plan red, and the top of the Job Admin & Inv tab, CALL ANTHONY IMMEDIATELY, with his number to tap, and the incident says the same, until somebody presses I called Anthony (by phone, by text or in person). An incident Anthony records on his own device owes no call.
  • The app sends nothing on its own: no text, no email. The call is yours to make.
  • Property damage offers I called Anthony too: the written report he is promised within 24 hours is this record, and the press says he has it.

A vulnerable adult

Florida law says that anyone who knows, or has reasonable cause to suspect, that a vulnerable adult is being abused, neglected or exploited reports it at once to the Florida Abuse Hotline, 1-800-962-2873 (§415.1034). That is you, in the house, not Anthony later.

  • What to watch for: unexplained bruises; somebody frightened of a family member or a carer; no food, medication or air conditioning; a helper who controls the money or will not leave you alone with the client; papers being signed that the client does not understand.
  • Record what you observed, not a diagnosis. Say on the report whether the hotline was called, or press Hotline called afterwards; the reminder stays red until then. Then call Anthony.
  • It stays internal. It is never on the Incident Record the client or their attorney receives, and never in the estate package. It prints on its own page, for Anthony only.

Afterwards

  • Add a note or Add photos as things develop: the carrier’s answer, the item found, what the mover said. Each is a new entry.
  • When it is dealt with, press Resolve and say what was done and how it ended. The app refuses a resolution with no note. Reopen if it comes back.
  • The job cannot close with an open incident. Close job names each one; Deposit Retained is refused the same way.
  • A claim can still move after the job closes. A note or the resolution recorded then is kept, marked as recorded after the close.
  • Void only one recorded in error (twice, or on the wrong client), with the reason. It stays on the record, struck through. Record it again where it belongs.

Where it ends up

The Incident Record is one page per job: every incident the client, their attorney or an insurer may read, each entry as written with who and when, the photographs linked, the resolution, and a footer that counts what the page lists. Print it from the card (Print Incident Record) or from More ▾ on Job Admin & Inv. It is filed to the client’s Incident Reports folder as the job closes, and from the card’s File to Drive at any time; on an estate it goes in the package to the attorney or the trustee, after the Disposition Ledger. The vulnerable adult concern prints on its own internal page, from the card only.

Tricky spots

The client says not to bother recording it.
Record it anyway, kindly. The record protects them as much as us, and the carrier will ask for it.
It happened yesterday and nobody recorded it.
Record it today with yesterday’s date. The day on the report is the day it happened; the app keeps the day it was written beside it.
I am not sure it counts.
Record it. A note resolves it and nothing is lost. The one that was not recorded is the one that gets asked about.
A family member broke it, not us.
Record it as property damage and say what you saw. The record is not blame; it is what happened, and the client is still told within 24 hours.
The mover damaged it on move day.
Property damage, the same day, with photographs. The claim against the mover is made against their own paperwork, the Bill of Lading and their inventory: see Move Day.
I recorded it on the wrong client.
Void it with the reason; it stays there, struck through. Record it on the right client.

Something went wrong

  1. The person first. 911 if in doubt. A hazard: everyone out of that area.
  2. Injury, missing item, vulnerable adult: call Anthony, (617) 650-6588. Then press I called Anthony.
  3. Photograph it where it lies. Wide, close, scale. Move nothing.
  4. Report an incident, today. Facts in order. No blame, no promises.
  5. Damage: tell the client within 24 hours. Press Client notified.
  6. Damage above $500: the carrier. Press Carrier engaged, with the claim number.
  7. Vulnerable adult: the hotline, 1-800-962-2873, then Anthony. Internal.
  8. A will or cash: Found on site. A firearm: the protocol.
  9. Nothing is edited. Add a note; Void with a reason.
  10. Resolve with a note. The job cannot close until you do.
