The First Night

Move-in, the first hour, and the check-in. The client arrives to a home, not to a wall of boxes.

For The concierge at the new home on move day, and after In the app Job Plan, Move day, and the client dashboard after the close Status Draft for Anthony Issued 17 Oct 2026

Before the truck arrives

  • Door and hallway widths were checked against the furniture plan before move-in day; a sofa that does not turn the corner is found out in the planning, not on the landing.
  • Floors protected before anything comes in. The floor plan the client approved is in your hand, and the movers place furniture by it.
  • Meet the move-in contact (the Director of Resident Services, the Social Worker or the Wellness Director in a community; the building manager otherwise). Get their direct line, not the front desk. This is the person the family calls with questions in the first weeks, so it goes on Edit Client under the destination.

The first hour

  1. A basic wipe-down of the bathroom and kitchen before anything is unpacked.
  2. Shower curtain up, toilet paper out, towels on the rail.
  3. Beds assembled and made, with the client's own bedding.
  4. The cooler and the first-night kit unpacked; the kettle or coffee maker working.
  5. The to-go bag and the personal things in from the car, straight to the bedroom. The medications found and put where they will live.
Rule 1

Bedroom, bathroom, kitchen

These three are finished before anything else is touched: daily essentials unpacked, furniture placed so the client can move safely, packing paper out of the room.

Rule 2

Familiar things do the work

Before the crew leaves: their own bedding on the bed, a few framed photographs where they can be seen from the bed and the chair, toiletries arranged the way they had them. The first night is the hardest, and familiar objects carry it.

Rule 3

Unpack in priority order, paced

Not everything on day one. The OPEN FIRST boxes, then the rest over the following days at a pace the client sets. Declutter again as you unpack: what does not earn its place goes.

Settle-in list

  • Groceries and essentials in; the dietary needs and preferences handed to the dining team in a community.
  • Smoke detectors checked. The locks changed or the spare keys made, if the client wants them. The keys and codes for the new home handed to the client and recorded on the Keys & access card.
  • The activities calendar in hand, a neighbour or two met, the address confirmed with the people and places that need it.
  • Packing debris collected: book the pickup before you leave.
  • Anything lost or damaged on the move is on the Incidents card already (Move Day). The claim is made against the mover's paperwork; the community is not responsible for a resident's belongings.

The check-in

The engagement ends at the handover, but one call about two weeks after the move is the difference between a client who settled and one who did not, and it is the call that earns the referral. The client dashboard reminds you from ten days after the move. Keep it short and specific: how the days are going, meals, sleep, getting around, anything they want changed, and whether they have met the people who run the place. Invite, do not insist.

When to say more. Distress that is severe, that lasts, or that comes with a sudden change in health or thinking is not homesickness. Say so to the family and point them to a doctor rather than waiting it out.

Move-in

  1. Floors covered. Floor plan in hand.
  2. Meet the move-in contact. Direct line.
  3. Wipe down, shower curtain, beds made.
  4. To-go bag to the bedroom. Medications placed.
  5. Bedroom, bathroom, kitchen first.
  6. Their bedding, their photographs.
  7. OPEN FIRST boxes opened, paper out.
  8. Keys and codes to the client. Record it.
  9. Debris pickup booked.
  10. Check-in call in two weeks.
