Estate Firearms Protocol

How a decedent’s firearms are documented, authorized, and moved to a licensed dealer — and the three things that never happen, on any job, for any reason.

Scope Non-NFA firearms only Transport by A. Graziano, sole authorized principal Status Draft for counsel review Issued 13 Sep 2026
Hard line 01

NFA items are never in our hands

A suppressor, short-barrelled rifle or shotgun, machine gun or AOW transfers directly from the estate to the dealer on ATF paperwork. Havellin does not take custody for one minute or one mile. There is no override and no exception for convenience.

Hard line 02

We never buy, and never take a cut

Havellin does not purchase estate firearms and takes no percentage of firearm proceeds. Our fee is our logged hours. That is what keeps us an agent of the estate rather than a dealer under 18 U.S.C. § 922(a)(1)(A).

The sequence

Order is load-bearing. Nothing physically moves before stage 3 exists in writing, and stage 3 cannot be drafted until stage 2 has produced serial numbers.

Ask on the intake call

Before anyone is at the property

  • Firearms is already the one red standing flag on the crew brief. Tick it and record the detail — a ticked row with no note reads the same as never having asked.
  • Ask what kind, in plain words: handguns, long guns, or anything that came with paperwork. Families rarely know the term NFA; they very often remember a framed certificate or “a stamp for the quiet one.”
  • Ask where they are kept and whether there is a safe, and get the combination from the representative rather than a family member.
  • Tell them plainly: do not move, unload or gather anything before we arrive. A well-meant tidy-up is how a loaded gun ends up in a box of linens.

Document in place

Day one, before anything is handled

  • Photograph where it lies before touching it — whole object, both sides, and a legible close-up of the serial number and every marking on the receiver.
  • Record it in the manifest as category Firearms. Serial number is the identity here, not our item number: the dealer’s receipt, an insurance claim and any police report will all be written serial by serial.
  • Run the NFA check below on every single item. Not the ones that look unusual — every one.
  • Verify unloaded: magazine out, action locked open, chamber checked by eye and by finger. Leave the action open with a chamber flag where the design allows.
  • If you are not completely confident an item is clear — an unfamiliar action, a jammed round, anything that does not feel right — stop and have the dealer collect that item. A wasted call-out costs a hundred dollars.
  • Ammunition is inventoried separately and never travels in the same container as a gun.

Get the authority in writing

Before anything leaves the property

  • The representative signs a transport authorization naming each firearm by make, model and serial, and naming the destination dealer. A blanket “dispose of the guns” is not this document.
  • It must state that title remains with the estate, that Havellin takes no ownership interest, that proceeds run to the estate, and that Havellin takes no share of them.
  • That paragraph is the whole basis of the arrangement. It is what makes the possession lawful under Fla. Stat. § 790.25(5), and what keeps a courier from reading as a dealer.
  • Record it on each item: Authorized By and Approval Date. The app already withholds any item missing either field.
  • Counter-sign and leave the representative a copy before you load the car.

The drive

One trip, direct

  • Locked hard case, in the trunk or cargo area. Ammunition in a separate locked container, ideally not on the same trip.
  • Never on your person. § 790.25(5) is explicit that a handgun carried under this subsection may not be carried on the person, and the safe reading covers everything in the case.
  • Direct route, no errands. No showings, no lunch, no school pickup — school zones carry their own rules and there is no reason to be near one.
  • Never leave the vehicle unattended with firearms in it. If that ever becomes unavoidable, the case is cable-locked to an anchor point — but the real rule is that it does not become unavoidable.
  • Intrastate only. Florida estate, Florida dealer. Do not cross a state line with estate property in the car.

Hand over at the counter

Before you leave the shop

  • Itemized receipt, serial by serial, signed and dated by the dealer, in your hand before you walk out. Twelve in the case and eleven on the receipt is a problem you cannot unwind six months later.
  • Photograph the receipt at the counter. Do not wait for the car.
  • Record which items are an outright purchase (with the price) and which are on consignment (with the terms and the expected timeline).
  • Confirm the dealer will remit to the estate, not to Havellin. Money never routes through us.

Close the record

Same day

  • Custody log entry per item: action, the dealer as the party, the date, method Transported by A. Graziano, and the receipt number. The log already merges correctly across devices — use it rather than a note.
  • Upload the signed authorization and the dealer receipt to the estate’s Drive folder.
  • Update disposition and channel on each item so the ledger and the court inventory reconcile.
  • Record proceeds against each item when they land, so the accounting shows what each object realized rather than a lump sum.

The NFA check

Run this on every firearm, every time. The risk was never mishandling one of these — it is failing to recognize it as one at all, in which case every gate downstream stays shut and the item rides in the trunk anyway.

Suppressor
A metal tube, often threaded, sometimes already fitted to a barrel and sometimes loose in a sock or a padded case. Any serial number on a bare tube is an NFA item.
Short-barrelled rifle
Rifle with a barrel under 16″, or under 26″ overall. If it reads as a rifle but looks stubby, measure it.
Short-barrelled shotgun
Shotgun with a barrel under 18″, or under 26″ overall. Common in older collections and rarely papered correctly.
Machine gun
A selector with any position beyond safe and semi-automatic. Also any loose part described as a conversion device.
Any Other Weapon
Pen guns, cane guns, smoothbore pistols, disguised firearms. Rare, but they turn up in exactly the kind of Palm Beach collection we work in.
A tax stamp
A sheet of paper with an ATF seal, often framed on the wall or folded in the safe beside the item. If there is a stamp, there is an NFA item, and it is registered to someone.

If in any doubt, treat it as NFA. The cost of being wrong in that direction is a phone call to the dealer. The cost of being wrong in the other direction is an unapproved transfer of a registered item under 26 U.S.C. § 5861, which carries up to ten years.

Never, on any job

  1. Never transport an NFA item. No representative’s authority reaches this, because it is not theirs to give — the registration is federal and the carve-out for estates names the executor, not the executor’s vendor.
  2. Never buy an estate firearm. Not once, not at fair value, not as a favour to a family that wants it gone.
  3. Never take a percentage of firearm proceeds. Bill the hours. A commission on a gun sale is the fact pattern that turns a concierge into an unlicensed dealer.
  4. Never let anyone else transport. Not Ashley, not a specialist, not a 1099 contractor. One named principal, or the dealer collects.
  5. Never hand a firearm to a family member or beneficiary — including the named heir, including one standing there asking for it. The dealer performs the transfer.
  6. Never transport anything you cannot verify is unloaded.
  7. Never cross a state line with estate firearms in the vehicle.

What we say to clients

Narrow and true. The independence line is the part worth leading with — it is the same promise the appraisal rule already makes, and it is what a trust officer is listening for.

Estimate, agreement and site copy

Firearms are documented in place and dispositioned through a licensed dealer. Where the representative authorizes it in writing, Havellin transports non-NFA firearms directly to that dealer for purchase or consignment.

Havellin never purchases estate firearms and takes no share of firearm proceeds — our fee is our time, so the estate’s interest and ours are the same one.

NFA items — suppressors, short-barrelled rifles and shotguns, and machine guns — transfer directly from the estate to the dealer on ATF paperwork. Havellin does not take custody of them at any point.

What we do not say

  • Do not market firearms handling as a service line. It is something we do within an estate engagement, not something we sell. A headline service is a fact pattern.
  • Do not use the word licensed anywhere near this. Havellin is insured and bonded. The dealer is licensed. That distinction is already a standing rule across every client document and it matters most here.
  • Do not promise an NFA timeline. A Form 5 to an heir currently clears in days, but a Form 4 to a dealer does not, and the representative will hold us to whatever we said.
  • Do not offer to value what we might move. If we are transporting to a buying dealer, the valuation opinion comes from someone else.

For counsel

Three questions. The research behind each is done; what is wanted is a yes, a no, or a redline on the authorization language.

Does the authorization carry the possession?

Havellin holds as the representative’s written agent, title never moving from the estate, delivering to an FFL. Does “in lawful possession” reach an agent holding under a personal representative’s statutory authority?

Fla. Stat. § 790.25(5) · Fla. Stat. § 733.607

Confirm the NFA line is where we have drawn it.

Our reading is that the estate carve-out names the executor, administrator or personal representative and does not extend to a vendor they engage, and that the temporary-conveyance exception reaches only a qualified dealer for repair or evaluation with return to the same possessor. Neither fits us.

27 CFR § 479.90a · 27 CFR § 479.11 · 26 U.S.C. § 5861

Does the fee structure keep us clear of dealing?

Havellin never purchases and takes no share of proceeds; compensation is logged concierge hours. Confirm that holds under the statutory test, and flag anything in the pending rulemaking that would change it.
