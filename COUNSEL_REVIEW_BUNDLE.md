**Assembled 2026-09-22. Nothing here has been reviewed by an attorney.**

## What this is, and how to use it

Every legal text in the Havellin app was drafted in-house under Anthony's standing
instruction — *"just come up with language and I'll get it reviewed. Do your best."* This
document is the accumulated list of everything that instruction deferred, in one place, so it
can be sent to counsel as a single engagement rather than five separate questions.

It is organised so an attorney can work down it: each item states **what the document
currently says**, **why it was drafted that way**, and **the specific question**. Where we
already believe we know the answer, that is stated — so counsel is confirming or correcting
rather than researching from nothing.

**⚠ Two things bound the drafting and should be flagged to whoever reviews this.**

1. **Primary sources were unreachable throughout.** The build environment's egress proxy
   blocks `flsenate.gov`, `leg.state.fl.us`, `law.justia.com`, `law.cornell.edu`, `ecfr.gov`
   and `atf.gov`. Every Florida and federal citation below was read through **secondary
   summaries**. The citations are believed correct and the *operative wording* has not been
   checked against the authority.
2. **Nothing here has gone to a real client.** Havellin is pre-launch. There is no signed
   agreement to unwind and no filed inventory to correct — which is the whole reason to do
   this now rather than after the first engagement.

## A. Estate / probate services agreement

### A1. The termination earn-out — is it compensation or a penalty? ⚠ PRIORITY 1

**What it says.** On a fixed-fee engagement, §8.1 provides that on termination for
convenience the client owes: the deposit, **earned in full on signature and not refundable**;
**75% of the fee** once the midpoint milestone is reached; the **whole fee** on completion of
the Exhibit A scope; plus any non-cancelable vendor commitments. No reference to hours worked.

**Why it was drafted this way.** The engagement bills a fixed price with a 20–35%
contingency, so measuring the exit in hours worked claws back exactly the upside the
contingency was paid for — Havellin absorbs the overrun and should keep the gain on an
efficient job. Anthony's opening position was that a client who terminates arbitrarily owes
**the full amount**; that was declined in drafting on the reasoning that a clause taking the
whole price regardless of work performed is tested as **liquidated damages**, survives only
if the figure is a reasonable forecast of actual loss **made at signing**, and the failure
mode is the bad one — a court that calls it a penalty **strikes the clause outright** and
drops us below what a smaller enforceable number would have collected. The stage-based
earn-out was drafted to read as compensation for milestones actually reached.

**The question.** Is the stage-based earn-out enforceable in Florida as agreed compensation
rather than liquidated damages? If it is properly analysed as liquidated damages, does it
survive? **And on a probate matter specifically** — the representative pays from estate funds
and the fee is subject to review — could an over-reaching exit clause taint the
reasonableness finding on the fee itself?

**Added 2026-09-23 — the home prep fee on a fixed-fee engagement.** When the job carries
home sale preparation vendors (painter, cleaner, landscaper and so on), Havellin's 30% fee on
those vendors now sits **outside** the fixed price, charged in addition to it on what the
vendors actually invoice; both forms state the two figures separately (standard §3.1, §3.3,
§3.5; the estate form's fee table and Fixed Project Fee paragraph). On termination the prep fee
is **earned on the preparation work completed or committed**, not in the flat fee's stages
(standard §12.2, estate §8.1), and the deposit stays earned on signature. **The question:** is
*committed* clear enough to collect on a vendor booked but not yet performed, and does a
percentage-of-vendor-spend fee owed on termination raise any issue the flat fee's earn-out
does not?

**Added 2026-09-30 (P16) — the hourly arm, on Anthony's answer.** Anthony: *"deposit paid, is lost."* The
estate form's hourly §8.1 said only *"The deposit is non-refundable if termination occurs after project start"*,
and *project start* is defined nowhere. It now reads, as two bullets: *"The deposit is earned in full on
signature of this Agreement and is not refundable. It is applied against the hours worked and materials used,
and no part of it is returned where it exceeds them"* and *"Where Havellin is in material breach of this
Agreement and has not cured that breach within seven (7) days of the Client's written notice describing it,
Havellin will refund any amount it holds above the hours worked and materials used through the date of that
notice."* **The question:** on an hourly engagement the deposit is half the estimate, so a client who stops in
the first week forfeits far more than the work done. Is that enforceable as a retainer earned on signature, or
is it tested as liquidated damages the way the fixed-fee earn-out might be? And the app also keeps a midpoint
payment on an hourly walkaway (it closes the job as *Deposit Retained*), which neither form calls earned:
should the clause say what happens to a payment above the deposit and the work done?

### A2. "For cause" — the definition, and an asymmetry between the two forms ⚠ PRIORITY 1

**What it says.** Cause is a **material breach by Havellin, described in the client's written
notice, uncured after seven days**. On the standard form this cross-references **§12.3**,
which carries notice-and-cure. On the estate form the mechanism is **stated inline**, because
that form's §8.2 is *Havellin's own* right to terminate and says nothing about cure.

**Why it was drafted this way.** If cause meant "the client is unhappy", every convenience
termination gets relabelled and the earn-out protects nothing. The asymmetry is deliberate: a
wrong cross-reference on a signed contract is worse than no cross-reference.

**The question.** Is seven days a defensible cure period for this kind of engagement? Is
stating the mechanism inline on one form and cross-referencing on the other a problem?

### A3. Seven-year retention — derived from nothing

**What it says.** Photographs, video and the written **Project Records** (the item-by-item
record naming who took what) are held for **seven years**.

**Why.** It was chosen to match the images. There is no analysis behind the number.

**The question.** What retention period is right for a Florida fiduciary engagement — and is
there a period after which holding this material becomes a liability rather than a protection?

### A4. Third-party names held for seven years

**What it says.** The Project Records name the **recipients** of estate property — a
daughter, a charity, an auction house — and are held for seven years under the same
confidentiality terms as the images, expressly outside the marketing clause.

**The problem.** Those recipients are **non-parties who never signed anything**. Their names
sit in Havellin's hands for seven years on the strength of a contract between Havellin and
the estate.

**The question.** Is that exposure acceptable as drafted? Does anything need to be added —
notice, a retention limit, or a right for a named recipient to object? *(Note: the
living-client form lets a client ask in writing that a recipient not be named; the estate form
deliberately does not, because a personal representative cannot suppress a beneficiary from a
fiduciary record. Confirm that distinction is right.)*

### A5. Trust arm — the §736.08135 carrying-value mechanics ⚠ PRIORITY 2

**What it says.** §5.2 *Florida Trust Administration Support* on a trust matter, replacing the
probate clauses. The Trust Schedule states date-of-death fair market value as the figure a
trust's first accounting opens with, and states on its face that it **supports rather than
constitutes** a §736.08135 accounting — listing what such an accounting contains that the
schedule does not (receipts, disbursements, gains and losses on sale, identification of
beneficiaries).

**Why.** Date-of-death FMV does three jobs at once — the IRC §1014 stepped-up basis, the Form
706 figure, and the trust's opening carrying value — and the §1014 step-up reaches revocable
trust assets. The "supports rather than constitutes" line is what keeps Havellin out of
fiduciary accounting work.

**The question.** Are the carrying-value mechanics stated correctly? **And is naming
§736.0813 alongside §736.08135 the right pair**, or is one of them wrong for this purpose?
*(Read through secondary summaries only.)*

### A6. Does the schedule read as an accounting? ⚠ PRIORITY 2

**The question.** Given A5, does the Trust Schedule as drafted leave a successor trustee or
their counsel entitled to treat it as an accounting, or as a substitute for one? The intent is
that it plainly does not.

### A7. Crew confidentiality — a promise about a hiring practice

**What it says.** §7 commits Havellin to *"Engage every team member and contractor under a
written agreement carrying confidentiality obligations, executed before they work on any
Havellin engagement."*

**Why.** It replaced a promise to *"require all team members and contractors to sign a
Non-Disclosure Agreement before accessing the property"* — a per-job NDA the firm does not
perform. Anthony confirmed: *"yes, everyone signs the 1099 before they work."*

**The question.** Does the 1099 independent-contractor agreement in fact carry confidentiality
obligations adequate to this promise? **This is the one clause in either agreement that
depends on a hiring practice rather than on anything the app does** — if the practice changes,
the clause changes with it.

### A8. §4.2 — the change-order form inside the agreement, and what a change order costs ⚠ NEW 2026-09-25

**What it says now.** §4.2 *Change Order Documentation* is a blank form printed inside the
estate agreement. Until 2026-09-25 it carried *Additional Cost Estimate $* and *Revised Total
Estimate $* on every engagement. It now follows the billing basis:

- **Time and materials** — *Additional TC Hours*, *Additional PS Hours*, *Revised Estimated
  Hours*, and a *Billing* row: *"The additional hours are billed as worked, at the hourly rates
  in Section 3.1. This Change Order does not itself create a charge."*
- **Fixed price** — the same two hours rows, then *Price of This Change $ (the additional hours
  at the rates in Section 3.1)* and *Revised Fixed Project Fee $*.

**Why.** The two bases bill a change in scope differently, and the app's own change order has
said so since 2026-09-11 (T&M) and 2026-09-25 (fixed). On T&M the timesheet bills every hour
worked, so a price on the change order would charge the same work twice; the form inside the
contract was still inviting that figure. On a fixed price nothing else charges the added scope,
so the change order carries its price — the hours at the §3.1 rate card — and the final invoice
adds exactly that figure. The standard (living-client) form's fixed arm states **no hourly rate
anywhere**, which is why the app now prints the rates on the change order itself.

**The question.** (1) Is a T&M change order that authorises scope in **hours** and states no
price an adequate written agreement to the added charge, given that the price is only fixed when
the hours are worked? (2) On a fixed-price engagement, is pricing the change at the rate card
enough when the standard form's fixed arm never states those rates — or should the rate card be
added to that form's fixed arm so the client has seen the rates before signing a change order
priced from them?

### A9. The appraisals Exhibit A lists — §2, §5 and the §5.3 table ⚠ NEW 2026-09-30

**What changed.** Anthony decided (2026-09-30, Q20) that appraiser scheduling is no longer priced into every
estate quote: an appraiser is priced only when one is added to the estimate as its own line, on any
documentation tier. So an estimate on the *Inventory with values* or *Contents list* tier, where counsel
arranges the appraisals, can now list and price an appraiser in Exhibit A, and until today the agreement
said in bold beside it that the coordination of appraisals was not within the engagement. Where Exhibit A
lists one (and the tier is not *Inventory + appraisals*, which already puts every appraisal with Havellin):

- **§2 Scope of services** adds *"Havellin will coordinate the professional appraisals listed in Exhibit A
  (Art Appraiser); any other appraisal remains the responsibility of the Client and the estate attorney."*
  On the photograph-and-list tier the bold carve-out becomes *"Valuation of estate assets is not within this
  engagement"*, followed by that sentence.
- **§5.2** (probate): *"Havellin will coordinate the professional appraisals listed in Exhibit A (Art
  Appraiser) within the 60-day inventory deadline from Letters of Administration issuance. Any other
  appraisal is arranged by the estate attorney; Havellin will give the appraiser access to the property and
  be present for the visit on request."* The trust arm names *"the trustee or their counsel"* and the
  no-proceeding arm *"the Client"* in place of the attorney, without the deadline.
- **§5.3 authority table**: the row reads *"Arrange the appraisals listed in Exhibit A"* — *"Items in those
  categories; any other appraiser as scheduled by"* counsel.

**The question.** (1) Is the split clear on who answers for an appraisal that turns out to be needed but is
not listed? (2) On probate, does promising the listed appraisals *within the 60-day deadline* take on the
appraiser's own delay, where the old *"all required asset categories"* promise sat only on the top tier?
(3) Exhibit A names the appraiser by category (*Art Appraiser*), not by firm: is that enough, or should the
firm be named once engaged?

**Added 2026-09-30 (P16) — §5.3's row for an appraiser Havellin does not arrange.** Where Havellin arranges
none (no appraiser in Exhibit A, on any tier but *Inventory + appraisals*), §5.3 carries a row that admits
somebody else's appraiser. It read *"Admit an appraiser engaged by counsel"* / *"Counsel's instruction"* /
*"As scheduled by the estate attorney"* (*"the trustee's counsel"* on a trust-only matter) whatever §5.2
said, and on a matter with neither probate nor a trust §5.2 names the Client. It now names the party §5.2
names, in all three columns:

| Matter type | Action | Approval Required | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Probate, both, or not answered | Admit an appraiser engaged by the estate attorney | Instruction from the estate attorney | As scheduled by the estate attorney |
| Trust only | Admit an appraiser engaged by the trustee or their counsel | Instruction from the trustee or their counsel | As scheduled by the trustee or their counsel |
| Neither | Admit an appraiser engaged by the Client | Instruction from the Client | As scheduled by the Client |

**The question.** (4) Does each row name the right party for its matter type?

## B. Standard (living-client) services agreement

### B1. Two confidentiality standards in one contract

**What it says.** §9 binds Havellin to *commercially reasonable efforts* and permits
disclosure to any vendor who *needs it*. §10.1 holds Documentation Media and Project Records
to a materially narrower standard.

**Why it matters.** A contract stating both is construed against its drafter, and the
practical effect is that we operate under the stricter rule while believing we have the
looser one — or the reverse.

**The question.** Should §9 be tightened so the two agree, rather than relying on the specific
governing the general?

### B2. §12.4 — the deposit is earned on signature ⚠ PRIORITY 1

**What it says.** On a fixed fee the deposit is **earned on signature and is not refundable**.
It previously read *"if the deposit exceeds the amount owed, Contractor will refund the unused
balance"*, which on a flat fee stopped early handed most of the 50% back.

**The question.** Same as A1, on the living-client form.

**Added 2026-09-30 (P16) — the hourly arm too.** The hourly §12.4 read *"Upon termination, Contractor will cease
work, remove its personnel and equipment, and provide a final invoice. Any deposit will be applied to amounts due.
If the deposit exceeds the amount owed, Contractor will refund the unused balance less any non-cancelable
costs."* On Anthony's answer (*"deposit paid, is lost"*) it now reads *"Upon termination, Contractor will
cease work, remove its personnel and equipment, and provide a final invoice for the Services performed and costs
incurred through the termination date under Section 12.2. The deposit is applied against that amount. **The
deposit is earned on signature and is not refundable**, including where it exceeds that amount, except where
Client terminates under Section 12.3 for a material breach by Contractor that Contractor has failed to cure, in
which case Contractor will refund any amount it holds above the amount owed."* **The question:** A1's hourly
question, on the living-client form.

### B3. Marketing — opt-out rather than opt-in

**What it says.** Anonymised use is authorised **unless the client ticks a box declining it**,
and written notice at any time has the same effect *whether or not the box was ticked*. The
restrictions in §10.2(a)–(e) are the anonymity standard: no street address, no name of any
client, family member or heir, no identifiable likeness without a separate release, no
document or record bearing identifying information.

**Why.** The opt-in version put a required consent question in front of a client through an
e-signature field that could silently fail to render, so a rendering bug produced a blank
where a consent should be. Under an opt-out a field that fails to place lands on the
documented default, and the client's route out does not depend on any box rendering.

**The question.** Is an opt-out defensible for a residential client in Florida, given the
restrictions? Is the "written notice at any time" exit sufficient?

### B4. The same opt-out now sits on the ESTATE form

**What it says.** The estate form carried an outright prohibition until 2026-09-18; it now
carries the same opt-out.

**Why it was reversed.** `Fla. Stat. §733.607` puts possession and control of estate property
in the personal representative's hands and they may sell the contents outright — so
authorising an anonymised photograph of a sideboard is strictly less than selling the
sideboard. Estate sale companies publish room-by-room photographs with the address attached,
weekly.

**What survives of the argument against it:** an executor who skims the clause consents by
silence **on behalf of heirs who never saw the contract**.

**Two rules deliberately sit OUTSIDE the contract**, in the operating playbook, because a
contract term would bind us to a judgement we want to make case by case and would imply a
client can waive past them:
- Nothing is published until the house has **closed or been fully cleared** (an empty Palm
  Beach estate whose contents were posted last week is a targeting problem).
- A **contested matter is never published at all**, whatever the box says.

**The question.** Is the fiduciary-consenting-for-heirs point adequately answered by the
anonymity restrictions? **Should either operating rule be a clause instead?**

### B5. Home Prep — hours added after signing, at a rate the agreement states ⚠ NEW 2026-09-25

**What it says now.** The Home Prep for Sale arm of this form, on an engagement priced on the
preparation vendors alone, says in §3.3 in bold that *"No Transition Concierge or Property
Specialist hours are billed on this engagement"*, and now continues: *"Hands-on work Client asks
for after signing, such as decluttering, is billed only if Client signs a Change Order under
Section 3.8 stating the Transition Concierge hours; those hours are then billed as worked at
Contractor's Transition Concierge rate of $150/hour, in addition to the management fee."* ($185 on
a Premium Estate engagement — the clause prints the rate the estimate was priced at.) §3.8 gained the sentence that
cross-reference needs: *"Hands-on work Contractor is asked to do after signing is documented in a
written Change Order signed by both Parties."* Until then §3.8 spoke only of vendor work being
re-quoted.

**Why.** A prep job is often signed with vendors only, and the first day in the house can show
half a day of clearing before the painters can start. Until 2026-09-25 there was no route to bill
it: the estimate locks at signature, and a change order on that engagement billed nothing. The app
now lets a change order add concierge hours. The change order the client signs states the hours,
the rate ($150 an hour; $185 on a Premium Estate engagement) and what that comes to at the
estimated hours; the acceptance screen states the rate again; and the final invoice bills the
**logged** hours at that rate on top of the 30% fee. **The rate was first left to the change
order**, on the reasoning that a vendors-only contract prices no hours and should carry no rate
card. **Anthony reversed that the same day** — *"I think we should mention the hourly rates in the
home prep agreement"* — so §3.3 now states the **concierge** rate, once, attached to the
change-order route and never as the basis of the fee. It states no Property Specialist rate:
the same clause says in bold that none are billed, and the app refuses specialist hours on a prep
change order. The contract and the change order read one rate definition, so the change order now
restates the contracted rate rather than setting it.

**The question.** (1) Is a Change Order that states the hours and the rate, signed by both
parties, an adequate amendment of a contract whose §3.3 says in bold that no hours are billed —
now that the carve-out names exactly that route? (2) A prep agreement generated **before**
2026-09-25 carries the bold sentence with **no** carve-out: is a signed change order stating hours
and rate enough there too, or should the agreement be re-issued first? Pre-launch, so no client
holds one today. (3) ~~Should the prep form state the concierge rate at all?~~ **Answered by
Anthony, 2026-09-25: yes** — the wording is quoted above. The question for counsel is now only
whether that sentence does the job: does stating a rate for contingent hours, beside a bold
statement that none are billed as signed, read as a coherent fee term rather than a contradiction?


### B6. The fee clause now names the rush premium and the discount — both forms ⚠ NEW 2026-09-29

**What it says now.** On an **hourly** engagement, each form's fee clause (§3.3 on this form,
§3.1 under the fee table on the estate form) adds one sentence for each price adjustment the estimate
carries, and nothing when it carries none:

- *"At Client's request this engagement is scheduled on a priority basis, for which an
  expedited-delivery premium of twenty percent (20%) of Contractor's fees is charged, as
  itemized on the Estimate and applied to the fees actually billed."*
- *"A preferred-client discount of ten percent (10%) applies to Contractor's labor fees [and to
  the expedited-delivery premium charged on them], as itemized on the Estimate and applied to
  the labor fees actually billed."* (The bracketed words appear only on a rush job.)

The estate form says *Havellin* where this form says *Contractor*. The percentage is the one the
estimate was priced at, spelled out. On a **fixed fee** saved before 2026-09-30 neither sentence
appears: both adjustments are inside the fee the fixed-fee clause already states. From 2026-09-30 a fixed
fee carries them as lines, and the fixed-fee clause names them (B7).

**Why.** The estimate (Exhibit A) itemizes both as rows of their own, and neither form mentioned
either: a signed contract silent on two lines of the exhibit it incorporates by reference. On the
same day the estimate's description of the premium was corrected. It used to read *"A second
Transition Concierge and an expanded specialist crew working in parallel to compress the project
calendar"* on every rush job, although the premium adds nobody; it now reads *"Priority
scheduling to meet the timeline you requested"*, naming added crew only where the estimate really
staffs it.

**Also changed, smaller.** With no moving or packing materials package quoted, both forms now say
*"No moving or packing materials package is quoted on the Estimate, and none is billed."* They
used to print *"(None — $0)"* inside a sentence describing a package.

**The question.** (1) Is one sentence each enough to make the premium and the discount contract
terms, given the estimate itemizes both and is incorporated as Exhibit A? (2) Does *"to meet the
timeline you requested"* on the estimate, beside a premium the client pays for it, read as a
promise to finish by a date, and if so should the estimate or the clause say the premium buys
priority rather than a guaranteed completion? (3) *"Applied to the fees actually billed"*: on an
hourly job the premium and the discount both follow the hours actually worked, including hours
added by a change order. Is that stated clearly enough for a client who reads the percentage
against the estimate's total? (4) Does *"none is billed"* on materials stop Havellin billing a
package the job turns out to need, or does a change order cover it?

### B7. The premium and the discount on a FIXED fee, and the premium off the prep fee — both forms ⚠ NEW 2026-09-30

**What changed.** Anthony decided (2026-09-30) that on a fixed price the expedited-delivery premium and the
preferred-client discount are no longer folded into the flat fee: the fee is the price of the scope and the
two print as their own lines under it on the estimate and the invoices. So where the estimate carries them,
each form's fixed-fee clause (§3.3 on the standard form, the *Fixed Project Fee* paragraph on the estate form)
now adds one sentence each:

- *"At Client's request this engagement is scheduled on a priority basis, for which an expedited-delivery
  premium of twenty percent (20%) of the fixed price is charged in addition to it, as itemized on the
  Estimate."*
- *"A preferred-client discount of ten percent (10%) applies to the fixed project fee[, less the moving
  materials it includes][, and the expedited-delivery premium charged on it], as itemized on the Estimate."*
  (Each bracket appears only when it applies.)

He also decided the premium is never charged on the 30% Home Sale Preparation Fee. On an **hourly**
engagement that carries the fee, B6's premium sentence now reads *"…of Contractor's fees, other than the Home
Sale Preparation Fee, is charged…"*. A contract signed before 2026-09-30 is billed as it was quoted.

**The question.** (1) The fixed-fee clause calls the fee *firm* and then states a premium charged *in addition
to it*: does the clause still read as a fixed price, or should it state the total the client pays? (2) The
discount on a fixed fee is taken on the fee less the materials package inside it, which the estimate does not
itemize on a fixed price: is *"less the moving materials it includes"* clear enough for a client checking the
figure? (3) Is *"other than the Home Sale Preparation Fee"* enough to exclude that fee from the premium's base?

### B8. A change order on an expedited fixed-price job carries the premium — both forms ⚠ NEW 2026-09-30

each change order when it is raised, so a change order raised before today, or on a fixed fee signed before

### B9. A Home Prep change order that adds a preparation vendor ⚠ NEW 2026-09-30

**What changed.** Anthony decided (2026-09-30) that on a Home Sale Preparation engagement a change order may
add a preparation vendor found mid-job (a painter, say), as well as or instead of concierge hours (B5). The
client accepts it by typing their name under the change order. The acceptance reads *"… the client confirms
they have reviewed and agreed to this change in scope, and the addition of Painting to the preparation
vendors, billed to them directly at cost, with the 30% site management fee on what it actually charges."* The
printed change order names the vendor and its estimated cost, and says *"The vendor bills you directly, at
cost, and the 30% site management fee in your agreement is billed on what it actually charges — about $1,350
at the estimated $4,500. Coordinating the vendors remains covered by that fee. The other preparation vendors'
costs are unaffected by this change."*

**What the agreement already says.** On a standalone Home Prep engagement §3.5 charges the fee on *"the total
third-party vendor costs managed under this Agreement"*, and §3.8 has work added after signing quoted and agreed
in writing before it proceeds, so the agreement's wording did not change. The labour form's fee row for bundled
prep charged it *"on the home sale preparation vendors identified in Exhibit A"*; since P16 a change order can add a
vendor there too, and the row says so (below).

**The question.** (1) Does *"vendor costs managed under this Agreement"* plainly include a vendor the client

## C. Estate Firearms Protocol

The full protocol is `firearms-protocol.html` in the repository and is linked from the app;
it says on its face that it is a draft for counsel review. ⚠ **Several primary sources
(ecfr.gov, atf.gov, law.cornell.edu, flsenate.gov) were blocked during drafting.**

### C1. Non-NFA transport by a named principal ⚠ PRIORITY 3

**What the protocol permits.** Anthony personally may transport a **non-NFA** firearm from
the property to a licensed dealer, once written authority naming the firearm **by serial**,
the serial itself and the destination dealer are all on the record. The app enforces all
three and refuses without them.

**The reasoning.** `Fla. Stat. §790.25(5)` (securely encased in a private conveyance);
`Fla. Stat. §733.607` (possession and control in the PR, with a duty to take all steps
reasonably necessary for the protection and preservation of the estate); ATF treating
temporary custody where title and ultimate control remain with the owner as **not a GCA
transfer**; destination is an FFL, the most protected recipient in the statute.

**The question.** Is that chain correct as applied to a service firm acting for a personal
representative?

### C2. The NFA prohibition ⚠ PRIORITY 3

**What the protocol forbids absolutely.** Havellin never transports an NFA item — suppressor,
SBR, machine gun — under any authority. The app enforces this as the one gate with **no
override**, on the reasoning that there is no person with standing to unlock it.

**The reasoning.** `27 CFR §479.11` defines transfer broadly; its temporary-conveyance
exception reaches a qualified manufacturer or dealer, for the sole purpose of repair /
identification / evaluation / research / testing / calibration, and return to the same lawful
possessor — three separate misses for us. `27 CFR §479.90a` carves out *"an executor,
administrator, personal representative, or other person authorized under state law to dispose
of property in an estate"* — read as a **fiduciary category, not a vendor they engage**.
`26 U.S.C. §5861` is ten years.

**The question.** Is the §479.90a reading correct — specifically, that it does not reach a
contractor engaged by the fiduciary?

### C3. "Engaging in the business" — the two standing rules ⚠ PRIORITY 3

**What the protocol forbids.** Havellin **never buys an estate firearm** and **never takes a
percentage of firearm proceeds**. Havellin bills hours.

**The reasoning.** `18 U.S.C. §922(a)(1)(A)`; BSCA 2022 broadened the test to *"predominantly
earn a profit"*; ATF's 2024 presumptions are proposed for rescission but the statutory
definition stands. The test is keyed to purchase and resale, so a courier paid its logged
hours is clear — and those two rules are what keep it that way.

**The question.** Are those two prohibitions sufficient? Is billing hours for the handling
itself a problem?

### C4. Crew screening — only if this becomes a service line

Today one named principal carries, and the app has no concept of who is driving — that is a
document rather than a gate, which is the honest state. **If firearms handling ever becomes
something the crew does**, the screening question under `18 U.S.C. §922(g)` and
`Fla. Stat. §790.23` needs a real answer first.

### C5. Verify the operative text ⚠ PRIORITY 3

The protocol's six stages, seven "nevers", NFA recognition check and client-facing claim
language were drafted from secondary summaries. **The operative text needs verifying before
the protocol is adopted.**

### C6. A firearm going to a named person, through a licensed dealer ⚠ NEW 2026-09-30 · PRIORITY 3

**What changed.** A firearm the representative directs to a family member could never clear the transport gate:
the protocol hands a firearm to nobody but a licensed dealer, and nothing recorded a transfer *to* a person
*through* one. Anthony (2026-09-30): *"sure"*. The line keeps its disposition (*To a person*, the person in
Channel / Recipient) and records a **dealer route**: the licensed dealer it goes through, who runs the background
check and makes the transfer. With the route, written authority naming the firearm by serial and the serial on the
record, the named principal may carry it to that dealer exactly as C1 describes; an NFA item is offered no route.
The protocol (revised 30 Sep 2026) says so at Stage 3 (*"For a firearm going to a named person, it names that
person as well: the dealer makes the transfer to them"*, and a new bullet on recording the route), Stage 5 (the
dealer's record lists which items are *"held for transfer to a named person (the dealer runs the background check;
the person collects from the dealer, never from us)"*), Stage 6, and Never 05 (*"The dealer performs the transfer:
a firearm the representative directs to a named person goes to the dealer like any other, and the person collects
it there."*). The release approval request the representative signs adds *"A firearm going to a named person goes
to the dealer as well, and the dealer makes the transfer to that person, with the background check and the
paperwork; Havellin never hands a firearm to anyone."* and prints *"through <dealer>, a licensed dealer"* on the
firearm's line. **Proposed, not applied** (client copy): in *What we say to clients*, *"…transports non-NFA firearms
directly to that dealer for purchase or consignment"* would become *"…for purchase, consignment, or transfer to a
person the representative names."*

**The question.** (1) Does C1's chain hold where the dealer acts as the transfer agent for an heir rather than as
buyer or consignee? (2) Is a dealer transfer required for a distribution to an heir, or only the firm's chosen
practice? (3) Should the client-facing sentence change as proposed?

## D. Inventory, valuation and filing

### D1. Fla. Prob. R. 5.340 alongside §733.604

Two live instruments cover the same inventory; the app cites **only §733.604**. Citing both
appears more complete. **Confirm** — and confirm the rule adds nothing that changes what the
schedule must contain.

### D2. §733.604(3) is cited nowhere ⚠ this is the commercially interesting one

`Fla. Stat. §733.604(3)` gives a beneficiary the right to a **written explanation of how each
inventory value was determined**. The app already carries the fields — valuation source,
valuation basis / comps, and a link to the appraiser — and cites the subsection nowhere.

**The question.** Is that reading right? If so, this is the statutory hook that makes a stated
valuation basis a **sellable deliverable** rather than a nicety, and it should be named in the
agreement and on the schedule.

### D3. Treas. Reg. §20.2031-6(a) — which reading? ⚠ PRIORITY 4, NEW 2026-09-22

**What the app implements.** On an estate recorded as filing a Form 706, a lot line grouping
several articles under one value is flagged where the **implied per-article value exceeds
$100** — i.e. the reading *"a group of articles contained in the same room, **none of which
has a value in excess of $100**, may be grouped together"*. Exactly $100 is treated as inside
the cap. It flags and explains; it never refuses.

**The question.** Is that the operative wording, or is it *"the total value of which does not
exceed $100"*? **The two differ by orders of magnitude on any lot of many cheap articles** —
forty kitchen sundries at $800 is comfortably inside the first reading and far outside the
second. `ecfr.gov` and `law.cornell.edu` were both blocked.

*(Separately: the app's §20.2031-6(b) aggregate — articles of marked artistic or intrinsic
value totalling over $3,000 require an expert's appraisal under oath filed with the return —
is believed settled and is implemented. Confirm if convenient.)*

### D4. Attribution — estimate versus appraisal

**What the documents say.** Havellin states an **estimated fair market value with a stated
basis**; that is not a credentialed appraiser's signed opinion, and every document says which
of the two it is carrying.

**The question.** Is the wording sufficient to keep Havellin's figure from being taken as an
appraisal — on a court-reviewed matter, by a beneficiary, or by the IRS?

### D5. The §732.402 exempt-property notice

**What it does.** Where exempt-flagged property exceeds the $20,000 allowance, the Court
Inventory prints a **prompt to check** rather than an assertion that the claim fails — on the
reasoning that the allowance covers household furniture, furnishings and appliances, motor
vehicles are separate, and the app cannot tell which class a flagged item falls in.

**The question.** Is that framing right, and is the cap figure current?
